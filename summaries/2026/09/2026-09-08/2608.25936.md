# One Symptom, Three Levers: A Critical Review of On-Policy Self-Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.25936) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25936)


## One-sentence takeaway
This review argues that preserving reasoning diversity in on-policy self-distillation requires controlling token weighting, privileged information, and teacher dynamics, while acknowledging that their best combination remains untested.

## Problem and motivation
On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) uses a model’s own rollouts and a copy conditioned on privileged information to provide dense supervision without a larger teacher. Its promise is reduced generation cost, but the same information advantage can encourage shortcuts, suppress deliberation, and narrow reasoning diversity (§§1.2–1.3).

The authors identify a fragmented literature that describes related failures under different names and lacks controlled comparisons across proposed remedies. They focus on mathematical reasoning and review work available through August 2026 (Introduction).

## Approach and contributions
This is a critical narrative review, **not a new training method or experimental study** (Abstract; Introduction).

- **Three-lever framework:** Organizes interventions by signal geometry and token weighting, the nature of privileged information, and temporal dynamics—including separate treatment of teacher updates and changing information exposure (§3; Figure 4).
- **Failure-mode synthesis:** Distinguishes general entropy and self-training failures from privileged-information effects, including suppression of deliberation and reinforcement of already common solution paths (§2.2).
- **Evidence assessment and agenda:** Connects OPSD to learning using privileged information, proposes a partially supported information-risk taxonomy, and identifies evaluation controls and unresolved comparisons (§§1.4, 2.3–2.4; Table 1).

## Evidence and main results
All empirical results below are **reported from cited studies**, not produced by this review.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Generation savings do not establish cheaper optimization steps | Founding OPSD uses one rollout capped at 1,024 tokens versus GRPO’s eight capped at 16k. A separate comparison reports 20.6 seconds per OPSD step versus 11.2 for GRPO on Qwen3-8B with 8×H100 under an equalized-budget setting. | §1.3, p. 8 |
| Checkpoint selection affects reported accuracy | Qwen3-1.7B OPSD reaches best-checkpoint AIME25 accuracy of 43.9%, versus 41.1% at training end. | §1.3, footnote 3, p. 8 |
| Single-attempt success can improve while multi-attempt coverage falls | For Qwen3-8B, cited self-distillation results change pass@1 from 71.9 to 73.4 and pass@16 from 83.6 to 78.5. The benchmark is unspecified here. Token entropy is nevertheless higher than for the GRPO-trained model. | §2.2, p. 13 |
| Privileged information can hurt thinking models | Up to 17% relative degradation in avg@16 across five thinking models; the review says effects depend on model type and generation budget. Exact benchmark/model breakdown is absent. | §2.3, p. 16; Table 1 |
| Error-aligned feedback can outperform reference-solution conditioning | Under strict self-distillation at fixed model and data, aligned critiques improve avg@12 by 5.27 over reference-conditioned OPSD and 16.11 over GRPO; benchmark and model are not specified in this summary. | §2.3, p. 17; Table 1 |
| Some advertised selective-supervision gains remain hypothetical | A cited oracle analysis estimates a 10–15× signal improvement from selecting positively aligned tokens; the review explicitly says this has not become a demonstrated training gain. | §2.1, pp. 11–13 |

The evidence supports treating accuracy, diversity, generation volume, and compute as separate outcomes. It does not establish a universally best privileged-information type, weighting rule, or teacher schedule. Comparisons span different methods and protocols, including external-teacher distillation and settings beyond mathematics.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** The review is non-exhaustive and excludes systematic coverage of multimodal and agentic branches. Much of its evidence comes from recent preprints, predominantly one model family and relatively small models (Introduction; Conclusion).
- **Author-stated evaluation risks:** Small benchmarks, decoding variance, contamination, checkpoint selection, and model-family effects can inflate apparent progress (§§1.3–1.4).
- **Missing controlled comparisons:** No shared strict-self-distillation protocol compares all major information types; teacher-update comparisons are similarly limited (§§2.3–2.4).
- **Unresolved retention evidence:** The proposed forgetting ordering between SFT, dense self-distillation, and sparse RL is explicitly conjectural and contradicted by SDPO results under a different protocol (§2.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The conclusion’s categorical claim that privileged information helps only when reconstructible at inference is stronger than the review’s supporting evidence: its taxonomy is explicitly tentative, and effects vary with model type and generation budget (§2.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Connections to vision and robotics motivate experiments but do not themselves validate equivalent mechanisms in language models (§§2.3–2.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Which privileged information best preserves transferable reasoning?** The full comparison is missing (§2.3). Next experiment: compare answers, worked solutions, partial traces, plans, rubrics, and critiques on identical models and data with matched compute and pass@k reporting.
2. **Can an online token-weighting signal approximate useful gradient alignment?** Alignment is retrospective, while entropy is available online but not validated as its proxy (§2.1). Next experiment: compare entropy and disagreement weighting against uniform weighting and an offline alignment oracle.
3. **Which teacher update rule remains stable over long training?** Existing comparisons incompletely control privileged information (§2.4). Next experiment: hold information fixed and compare frozen, EMA, gated-refresh, and proximal teachers over runs long enough to expose delayed collapse.
4. **When does dense self-distillation forget more than sparse RL?** Reported findings conflict across training protocols (§2.4). Next experiment: compare SFT, OPSD/SDPO, and GRPO under matched single-task and sequential-task settings, measuring prior-task retention alongside new-task accuracy.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The review’s structure and stated uncertainties are clear, but empirical conclusions depend on summarized external studies.

Material information unavailable here includes detailed study-selection criteria, complete experimental configurations, consistent uncertainty estimates and seed counts, several result-specific benchmark/model identities, and unified ablations across the three levers. No new experiments or reproducibility package are reported.
