# The information geometry of large language models is shared, learned, and controllable

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11063) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11063)


## One-sentence takeaway
The paper reports that next-token Fisher–Rao geometry captures shared predictive structure across language models and enables interventions with lower measured output disturbance than matched Euclidean updates, while broader capability preservation remains unestablished.

## Problem and motivation
Activation distances depend on hidden-state coordinates: an invertible reparameterisation can change those distances without changing model behaviour. The author therefore asks what geometry predictive behaviour identifies, how language statistics shape it, and whether it can guide selective behavioural changes. The proposed gap is a unified account connecting cross-model agreement, learning dynamics, and intervention cost (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Behaviour-based geometry:** Compare models through pairwise Fisher–Rao distances between next-token distributions on common prompts. The author gives conditional identification and convergence results, then tests them with read-out perturbations and randomised synthetic-language assignments (Results, pp. 2–5).
- **Shared structure and human predictions:** Separate consensus geometry from model-specific residuals; evaluate semantic-category transfer, human completion agreement, and calibration learned solely from model distributions (pp. 5–6; Supplementary Note 19).
- **Statistical origins:** Predict spectral structure and effective dimension using token probabilities and read-out directions; separately forecast fact acquisition from corpus n-gram margins and manipulate evidence depth experimentally (pp. 6–10).
- **Selective control:** Pull output geometry back to activations as \(G=J^\top HJ\), then use damped updates proportional to \((G+\alpha R)^{-1}q\). Contributions include predicting the advantage over Euclidean control, reference-prompt metrics for reusable updates, and applications to editing, attribution, dictionary learning, and LoRA (pp. 11–14).

Natural-gradient steering is explicitly acknowledged as prior work; the claimed contribution is its broader theoretical integration, predictive tests, and extensions—not invention of the underlying natural gradient (p. 2).

## Evidence and main results
All findings below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Output geometry agrees more strongly across models | On natural text, mean Spearman agreement is **0.88**, versus **0.62** for mid-layer and **0.61** for last-layer activation geometry; ten models spanning transformer, state-space, and recurrent architectures. | pp. 4–5; Fig. 2a |
| Assigned language laws determine learned geometry in controlled systems | Across eight synthetic-language pairs, three architectures, and two capacities, changing the law recovers **over 99%** of imposed squared geometric separation at matched predictive accuracy. | p. 4; Methods, p. 17 |
| Shared geometry supports category transfer | Eight-way topic accuracy: **0.659** across models, **0.700** using consensus geometry, **0.724** within model, and **0.125** chance; five-fold evaluation using distances to fixed anchors. | Supplementary Note 19, p. 85 |
| Model-only calibration improves coarse human prediction in most tested families | On DERCo’s five fixed narratives, expected human log-score gains over native laws are **0.0196–0.0507 nats** in six of seven families; Qwen2 declines by approximately **0.0030 nats**. Outcomes are 64 semantic bins plus a residual bin. | Extended Data Fig. 2d; Supplementary Note 19.4 |
| Token/read-out profiles predict spectra without fitted parameters | Nine external models, 64 held-out contexts each: family-median normalised effective-dimension RMSE **0.0270–0.0521**. In a separate matched comparison, probability-only profiles better predict effective dimension, while weighted profiles better predict spectral exponents in every tested family. | Fig. 3; pp. 6–9 |
| Corpus statistics forecast acquisition | Fresh-fact trajectories across Pythia 70M, 160M, and 410M achieve **R² 0.775–0.792** and timing errors **0.77–0.96 log₂ training-step units**, beating constant-time and label-permutation baselines without refitting. | Fig. 4a |
| Evidence depth causally delays persistent acquisition | Deep versus shallow evidence shifts the acquisition-time 10th–20th percentiles by **2.10 log₂ steps**, approximately **4.3×** training steps. A separate three-architecture, two-language experiment finds **1.75–2.86×** delays, with all six confidence intervals excluding zero. | Extended Data Fig. 4; Methods, p. 21 |
| Local geometry predicts intervention advantage | Across **3,515 usable matched-response measurements**, 11 models, three objectives, and three depths, the median measured/predicted cost ratio is **0.967**; log–log slope **0.982**. | Fig. 5a |
| Reusable updates transfer while reducing reference disturbance | Four base models; four donor and four reference prompts per objective; eight unseen targets. All **24** mean target effects are positive. Euclidean/reference-Fisher sequence-KL ratios are **2.90–6.29** at matched donor effects. | Fig. 5c–d; Supplementary Note 10.5 |
| Matched editing and feature-ablation results favour geometric measures | On 30 GPT-2 CounterFact edits, both methods reach **+5 log-odds**, with **10.57×** lower geometric-mean other-token KL for Fisher updates. On 303 Pythia-410M SAE ablations, predicted Fisher cost correlates with exact KL at **ρ=0.997**, versus **0.896** for activation magnitude. | Table 1; Extended Data Figs. 5–6 |
| Fine-tuning improves local preservation | Pythia-70M rank-4 LoRA, eight seeds, three matched training gains, and 16 held-out prompts: tuned AdamW+KL incurs **1.57×** the preservation-KL frontier AUC of exact natural gradient, **95% interval 1.14–1.98**. | Extended Data Fig. 7 |

The evidence combines conditional theory, observational comparisons, and randomised causal tests. Causality is supported for the controlled language assignments and evidence-depth manipulations. Cross-model agreement and human alignment are observational findings. Lower KL establishes distributional preservation on the measured prompts; it does not by itself establish safety, general capability retention, or equivalent free-generation behaviour.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Incomplete source:** The supplied text is truncated, omitting substantial supplementary material, including portions of the theoretical derivations. This analysis does not provide complete coverage.
- **Local guarantees:** Minimum disturbance applies to a regularised local objective. Prediction accuracy deteriorates with larger interventions, and cumulative path KL differs from endpoint or unrelated-prompt disturbance (Extended Data Fig. 8; Supplementary Note 10.4).
- **Reusable-control scope:** Preservation is evaluated on the reference prompts used to construct the metric; unseen target effects are measured but not matched. **Analyst inference:** preservation on new reference distributions remains an important untested boundary of this experiment (Supplementary Note 10.5).
- **Spectral approximation:** Top-512-token spectra retain approximately **0.90** probability mass on average, below the intended **0.99** target; the author reports a full-vocabulary check for the core spectral fit (Methods, p. 20).
- **Human-comparison scope:** Semantic embeddings and coarse outcome bins do not establish individual-word recovery. Full-law reconstruction bounds remain non-positive, and DERCo intervals are conditional on five stories (Supplementary Note 19).
- **Comparability and breadth:** Query-time routed editing is not a controlled ranking against persisted editors. LoRA task-transfer and capability-retention endpoints did not support broader conclusions; dictionary recovery uses planted features (pp. 24–25; Supplementary Notes 13 and 16).
- **Reproducibility and scale:** Code, derived data, and detailed prompts are available on request rather than through a supplied public release. Full GPU-resident pullback solves are reported through 2.8B parameters; larger-model evidence is more restricted (pp. 25–26; Supplementary Note 20).

## Open research questions
1. **Does reusable control preserve behaviour outside its reference set?** Current preservation measurements reuse optimisation reference prompts. Next experiment: evaluate disjoint reference prompts and free-running continuations while matching achieved target effects.
2. **Do KL improvements translate into capability or safety retention?** The LoRA experiment explicitly does not establish this. Next experiment: compare natural gradient and tuned KL-regularised optimisation at matched task gains on sufficiently sensitive capability and safety evaluations.
3. **Does evidence depth predict acquisition in natural pretraining causally?** Randomisation establishes the effect in synthetic languages; real-checkpoint forecasting remains observational. Next experiment: manipulate controlled factual evidence within otherwise matched natural-text training corpora.
4. **How well does control scale computationally?** The width-independent bound concerns solver iterations, not total runtime. Next experiment: measure runtime, memory, and matched-effect preservation on larger models using full-vocabulary and approximate operators.
