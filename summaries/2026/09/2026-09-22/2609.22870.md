# Towards Full Pipeline FP8 Reinforcement Learning for LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.22870) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22870)


## One-sentence takeaway
Calibrated Clipping uses periodic BF16 references to stabilize FP8 reinforcement learning and recover much of its lost accuracy, although BF16 parity varies by setting and end-to-end speedup is not measured.

## Problem and motivation
FP8 can accelerate LLM reinforcement learning, but quantizing both rollout and training still produces entropy surges and garbled responses despite truncated importance sampling (TIS). The authors identify a gap beyond rollout–training mismatch: quantization distorts policy importance ratios, incorrectly clipping negative-advantage tokens and removing gradients that should suppress bad outputs (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Failure diagnosis:** BF16 shadow passes and clipping analysis link compounded ratio error to excessive lower-bound clipping. Relaxing that bound prevents the entropy surge but produces premature entropy saturation, motivating a balanced correction (§3).
- **Calibrated Clipping:** Match the fraction of negative-advantage tokens clipped under FP8 to its BF16 reference, then adjust the upper bound to match the BF16 ratio of positive to negative objective contributions (§4).
- **Periodic implementation and evaluation:** Two forward-only BF16 shadow passes supply reference ratios every 20 training steps; bounded, smoothed updates limit abrupt changes. Experiments cover GRPO, DAPO, three FP8 scaling granularities, mathematical reasoning, and coding (§§4–5; Appendix A.4).

The evidence supports useful stabilization and consistent accuracy improvements over vanilla FP8 in the tested settings. It does **not** establish universal BF16 equivalence, statistical significance, or the claimed practical end-to-end efficiency benefit after calibration overhead. The authors’ broad “BF16-level performance” conclusion is stronger than the DAPO and coding comparisons.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Analysis targets token-level GRPO/DAPO clipping; sequence-level calibration remains future work (§6).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism:** Diagnostics and the lower-bound intervention support the proposed failure mechanism, but matching clipping fractions does not guarantee that the same individual tokens retain gradients (§§3–4).
- **Analyst inference—ablation coverage:** The paper tests fixed lower-bound relaxation and calibration interval/initialization sensitivity, but does not isolate dynamic lower-bound alignment from upper-bound rebalancing in a matched component ablation (Figure 5; A.5).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Evidence is confined to Qwen models and mathematics/coding. CISPO and BAPO failures are shown in one rowwise-FP8 setting, limiting broader comparisons (A.2–A.4).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** §5 says all reported metrics use the final half of training, whereas §5.1 describes best-average checkpoint selection. Their precise interaction is unclear. Run counts, uncertainty estimates, and hardware details are absent.

## Open research questions
- **What is the net efficiency gain?** Calibration passes are excluded from throughput measurements. Measure complete rollout–update wall time, including shadow passes, at matched achieved accuracy.
- **Which calibration component drives recovery?** Existing interventions do not fully separate the two stages. Compare lower-only, upper-only, and combined dynamic calibration under identical settings and multiple seeds.
- **Does calibration transfer to sequence-level objectives?** The authors explicitly leave this open (§6). Measure quantization-induced sequence-ratio distortion, then test a corresponding BF16-referenced calibration.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and tabulated gains are clear; causal completeness, generality, and practical efficiency are less established.

Material missing information includes repeated-run variability, hardware and detailed throughput configuration, measured calibration overhead, end-to-end timing, coding evaluation protocol, and a code/checkpoint release location. Some figure contents are not recoverable from the supplied text, including the garbled-response example.
