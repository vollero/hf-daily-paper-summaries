# CARE: Experience-Guided Atomic Corrective Execution for Vision-Language-Action Policies

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.24118) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24118)


## One-sentence takeaway
CARE combines failure-informed corrective demonstrations with geometric monitoring to improve VLA manipulation success, while controlled recovery evaluations show benefits from its execution mechanism but persistent difficulty with structural failures.

## Problem and motivation
VLA policies trained mainly on successful demonstrations can struggle after slips, misplaced objects, or coordination errors move execution into unfamiliar states. The authors identify two gaps: corrective-data collection often uses generic perturbations rather than observed failure patterns, and nominal-start benchmarks do not isolate recovery ability (§I–II).

## Approach and contributions
- **Experience-guided corrective data:** Fit stage-specific distributions to translation and yaw deviations from failed rollouts, sample perturbations, let physical dynamics produce failure states, and collect short expert corrections through simulation planning or real-world teleoperation (§III-B; §VIII).
- **Atomic corrective execution:** A one-time GPT-4.1 plan specifies stages and corrective instructions. An event-triggered monitor uses SAM 3 masks, Depth Anything 3 estimates, and geometric predicates to select adjustments or repeated operations. One VLA executes both nominal and corrective actions (§III-C; §IX).
- **FSR-Bench:** Introduces 36 intermediate-failure scenarios across five tasks, separating local deviations from structural failures. Shared corrective training data use uniform perturbations; shared evaluation states come from failure distributions estimated using a mixture of nominal policies. This evaluation isolates execution rather than experience-guided synthesis (§III-D; §X).

## Evidence and main results
All improvements below are absolute percentage points; reported averages are over the corresponding task sets.

The strongest component evidence comes from matched-data FSR-Bench comparisons and the sampling-distribution ablation. Standard task comparisons establish benefits of the combined system, rather than execution alone. Improvements are not universal: π₀-FAST Handover Block success falls from **24% to 16%** with CARE (Table I). Hard-regime recovery also remains low across backbones (Table III).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-described scope:** Failure modeling uses yaw because it dominates the studied tasks; new atomic skills require new predicate templates, and execution operates within a fixed initial plan (§VIII–IX).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Independent scalar deviation fits may miss coupled errors; the paper does not compare them with joint distributions or full rotational modeling (§III-B).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation breadth:** Physical evaluation covers one dual-arm platform and four tasks. FSR-Bench tests predefined recovery scenarios, so broader failure coverage is unestablished (§IV-A; §X).
- **Analyst inference—causal limits:** The rollback comparison changes corrective supervision as well as recovery strategy, preventing clean attribution to the strategy alone (§XI-D; Table XI). Intervention counts likewise do not isolate the necessity of adjustments versus re-operations; the authors explicitly acknowledge this distinction (§XI-A).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** No confidence intervals or repeated-training-seed variability are reported. The claim that empirical sampling better captures long-tail failures is an interpretation of success improvements, without a direct tail-coverage comparison (§V-B).
- The authors provide no dedicated limitations section; reinforcement-learning integration is proposed as future work (§VI).

## Open research questions
- **Would joint, full-pose failure models improve recovery?** Current fits model scalar translation components and yaw separately. Compare joint pose models against the existing prior under identical rollout and corrective-data budgets (§III-B).
- **What limits structural recovery?** Hard-regime RSR remains low. Compare the fixed planner with online replanning while holding training data and monitoring constant, and categorize unrecovered failures (Table III).
- **How robust is the monitor outside calibrated conditions?** Predicate reuse is demonstrated, but transfer to new skills and embodiments is not established. Test held-out skills or a second robot while measuring false positives, false negatives, and task success (§IX; §XI-E).
