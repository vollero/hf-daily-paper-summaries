# GameHorizon Suite: Multi-Horizon Data and Evaluation in Gameplay

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.25001) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25001)


## One-sentence takeaway
GameHorizon combines aligned gameplay videos, human controls, and hierarchical instructions with offline and online evaluation, reporting improved action planning from longer-horizon instructions but validating online transfer only in Minecraft.

## Problem and motivation
Gameplay requires both decomposing long-term goals and executing precise controls. The authors argue that existing datasets lack sufficient game diversity or dense language annotations, while small, harness-dependent online evaluations make model comparisons difficult and obscure failure causes (§1). Their proposed gap is a common evaluation framework spanning perception, planning, and action across model families.

## Approach and contributions
- **GameHorizon-Annotator:** Segments recordings using control events and VLM refinement, then progressively merges and annotates clips as operations, goals, and strategies. Dynamic programming enforces respective durations of 1–5 seconds, 1–2 minutes, and 5–8 minutes (§3.2; Fig. 3).
- **GameHorizon-Data:** Provides synchronized videos and directly recorded keyboard–mouse controls across 21 games, with dense hierarchical instructions on filtered footage. The authors claim novelty in combining scale, AAA focus, direct human actions, and multi-horizon annotations (§3.3; Table 2).
- **GameHorizon-Bench:** Combines four-option offline questions about actions, goal decomposition, and cross-horizon consistency with Minecraft tasks that reset failed subtasks to permit subsequent diagnosis. End-to-end success still requires completion without failures or resets (§§3.4, 4.1).

The contribution is principally data infrastructure and evaluation, rather than a new trained gameplay policy. Asset release is promised in the abstract; completed availability is not established consistently by the text.

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large aligned corpus, with partial instruction coverage | 5,000 recording hours from 100 players across 21 games; 4,341 hours retained for annotation, containing 6,184,036 instructions. Controls cover all recordings. | §3.3 |
| Offline benchmark separates models | Among 44 question-answering models, mean accuracy across three primary tasks was 64.7%, versus 25% random chance; model averages ranged from 44.6% to 80.2%. Each primary task contains 1,000 questions. | §§4.1–4.2; Table 4 caption |
| Fine-grained action task is hardest | Across those models, mean accuracy was 57.3% for action selection, 65.1% for decomposition, and 71.6% for cross-horizon consistency. | §4.2 |
| Instructions help different capabilities differently | Across four models, with 200 questions per diagnostic variant, short instructions raised current-action accuracy from 44.5% to 58.6%. Adding multi-horizon context raised future-action accuracy from 34.8% with short instructions to 41.8%; the reported gain over frames alone was 7.2 percentage points. No annotation fine-tuning was used. | §§1, 4.3; Table 11 caption |
| Abstraction is easier than decomposition | Across the four diagnostic models, bottom-up abstraction averaged 98.4% accuracy versus 69.5% for top-down decomposition, on 200 questions per variant. | §4.3; Table 12 caption |
| Dedicated-agent transfer is limited in this protocol | JARVIS-VLA scored 54.2% on in-domain VPT Minecraft after fine-tuning, versus 28.1% on unseen AAA action questions; zero-shot Gemma 4 31B-IT scored 54.1% on the Minecraft comparison. Agent actions were converted to answers by sequence alignment. | §§4.1–4.2; Tables 5–7 captions |
| Online rankings align, but full-task success remains low | For 12 models selected across offline tiers, tiers mapped to successive groups of three online ranks. On 20 Minecraft tasks comprising 62 subtasks, GPT-6-Astra achieved 45.0% end-to-end success; ten models achieved at most 10.0%, under a 400-call budget per subtask. | §§4.1, 4.4; Table 15 caption |

These findings support benchmark discriminability and the usefulness of hierarchical instructions for the tested offline questions. The online association supports relevance to the sampled Minecraft setting; it does not establish reliable prediction of AAA gameplay performance. ## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Online evaluation uses Minecraft because underlying states in most AAA games are inaccessible. Cost restricts online testing and diagnostic model coverage (§§3.4, 4.1, 4.3).
- **Author-stated ambiguity:** Abstract goals may admit multiple valid operation sequences, and bottom-up abstraction approaches ceiling accuracy (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference—annotation validity:** No independent human annotation audit or quantitative segmentation ablation is supplied. Strong performance on derived abstraction questions does not independently establish label correctness.
- **Analyst inference—comparison fairness:** Dedicated agents use sequence alignment, potentially reject all options, and retain their native context windows; question-answering models select options directly. Scores therefore reflect both capability and interface differences (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—construct validity:** Recognizing a recorded sequence among distractors is narrower than generating and executing a valid plan. The acknowledged multiplicity of valid plans makes distractor validation particularly important (§§3.4, 4.3).
- **Analyst inference—statistical support:** No confidence intervals, correlation coefficient, or clearly specified repeated-rollout protocol accompanies the reported offline–online association.

## Open research questions
1. **Does offline accuracy predict performance beyond Minecraft?** Current online evidence is single-game. Next experiment: repeat matched offline and online evaluation across several controllable AAA environments.
2. **How accurate are the hierarchical annotations?** Independent annotation-quality measurements are unavailable. Next experiment: have blinded human reviewers audit segmentation, action grounding, and goal validity at each horizon.
3. **Do hierarchical instructions improve executable policies?** Reported gains concern offline inputs without fine-tuning. Next experiment: train matched agents with short-only versus multi-horizon supervision and compare held-out online success.
4. **How much do answer format and distractors drive rankings?** Model families use different answer interfaces, and goals can have multiple valid realizations. Next experiment: compare MCQs with free-form planning and execution while auditing alternative valid answers.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the suite design and headline reported findings, but leaves material gaps: most numerical table bodies, exact figure-embedded prompts, annotation audits, detailed distractor validation, sequence-alignment thresholds, online harness and repetition details, uncertainty estimates, and confirmed release status.
