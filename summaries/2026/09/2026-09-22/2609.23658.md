# Why Do Video Diffusion Models Violate Physics? Unveiling the Flaws in Attention Mechanisms

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23658) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23658)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors identify spatial anchoring from rotary position embeddings as a contributor to implausible motion in Wan2.1 and report improved human-rated physical commonsense after reducing spatial RoPE frequencies during early denoising (Sections 5–6).

## Problem and motivation
Video diffusion models can generate visually convincing videos with impossible object motion. The authors investigate how trajectories form internally, arguing that prior observations of early trajectory formation leave its mechanism unexplained. Their analysis primarily targets solid dynamics, where object trajectories are easier to track (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Motion-planning analysis:** Combine object-token cross-attention maps, trajectory convergence metrics, approximate attribution patching, and head ablations to distinguish trajectory-shaped attention from heads that materially affect motion (Section 4; Appendix E).
- **Candidate-region mechanism:** Extract competing object locations and measure their mutual self-attention. The authors propose that RoPE favors spatially aligned locations across frames, allowing early commitments to suppress plausible moving trajectories (Section 5).
- **RoPE intervention:** Reduce height/width RoPE frequencies during the first five denoising steps. Evaluate both inference-only modification and attention-only LoRA adaptation with training concentrated on early denoising (Section 6; Appendix H). The authors claim the first interpretability study of this motion-planning process; priority cannot be verified from the supplied text.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Trajectories emerge early | For the main Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B basketball case, seed 26, attention metrics turn around step 5 of 50; maps show a clear trajectory by step 7. | Section 4.1; Figures 2–3 |
| Attention patterns alone do not identify motion-driving heads | Ablating high-convergence, high-contribution heads collapses the trajectory; ablating trajectory-shaped, low-contribution heads largely preserves it. Ablations apply across all denoising steps by default. | Section 4.2; Figure 4; Appendix E.2 |
| Inference-only scaling improves benchmark ratings | On 343 VideoPhy prompts, seed 42, blinded human evaluation: base → modified RoPE gives overall SA **53.64% → 57.43%**, PC **29.45% → 39.94%**; evaluation scaling is 0.70. | Table 1; Appendix H.2 |
| Fine-tuning plus scaling improves over listed training baselines | Same benchmark/protocol: LoRA + modified RoPE achieves **64.72% SA / 41.98% PC**, versus LoRA **55.69% / 35.57%** and VideoREPA **56.56% / 37.90%**. | Table 1 |
| Prompt refinement and scaling combine effectively | Same benchmark/protocol: prompt refinement alone gives **80.47% SA / 44.31% PC**; modified RoPE + refinement gives **86.30% / 58.89%**; LoRA + modified RoPE + refinement gives **87.46% / 62.68%**. | Table 1 |
| Benefits are not uniform | On VideoPhy fluid–fluid cases, inference-only modified RoPE lowers SA from **63.74% to 61.87%**, while increasing PC from **36.55% to 43.74%**. | Table 1 |
| Adaptive scaling is unresolved | Learnable head- and timestep-dependent scaling produces no significant physical-commonsense improvement according to the authors; numerical performance is not supplied. | Appendix H.1.3 |

These results support a useful intervention in the evaluated model family and a plausible attention-based explanation. They do not establish that RoPE explains most physical failures generally, or that better benchmark ratings imply learned physical laws.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-reported:** Inference-only scaling needs manual tuning for individual basketball cases and can leave motion and object-permanence instability. Image-to-video and other architectures remain future work (Figure 22; Section 7).
- **Analyst inference—mechanistic scope:** Detailed analysis centers on simple motions and a small collection of examples. Reference trajectories and “winner” labels derive from the model’s final attention maps, not independent physical ground truth (Sections 4–5; Algorithms 1–2).
- **Analyst inference—causal interpretation:** The RoPE visualization omits content-dependent query/key coefficients, using a cosine-kernel proxy. It supports a positional tendency, not a universal account of actual attention (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation uncertainty:** VideoPhy uses one generation seed. Evaluator counts, agreement, confidence intervals, and quantitative aesthetic results are absent. The 14B evidence consists of qualitative examples (Appendix H.2–H.3).
- **Analyst inference—attribution and reproducibility:** Numerical ablations do not isolate scaling from the custom timestep sampler. Section 6.2 describes ramping scaling during training, whereas Appendix H.1.3 describes a fixed value; the checkpoint-selection evaluation split is also unspecified.

## Open research questions
- **Does spatial anchoring predict failure across architectures?** Evidence is concentrated on Wan. Repeat candidate-region analysis and matched RoPE interventions in other video architectures.
- **Does scaling rescue physically correct candidates?** Current winner labels track eventual generation outcomes. Test simulated motions with known trajectories and measure whether interventions redirect attention toward physically valid locations.
- **Which training change produces the gains?** Scaling and early-timestep emphasis are combined. Run a factorial ablation of scaling, sampler, and LoRA across multiple generation seeds.
- **Can scaling be selected automatically?** Manual tuning remains useful, while learned scaling failed. Compare fixed scaling with motion-sensitive objectives or candidate-competition signals on held-out prompts.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the reported benchmark gains and analytical procedure, but the broader root-cause claim remains less established.

Material missing information includes human-rating reliability, multi-seed benchmark uncertainty, numerical component ablations, quantitative 14B and aesthetic scores, the checkpoint-selection split, and an unambiguous training-scaling schedule. Code availability is author-reported and was not independently checked.
