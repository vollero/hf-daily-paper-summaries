# UltraTex: Unleashing 2K Multi-View Diffusion for 3D Texturing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23169) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23169)


## One-sentence takeaway
UltraTex combines foreground token removal, sparse attention, and adapted VAE decoding to generate 2048-resolution multi-view textures with large reported speedups, although missing numerical table contents prevent verification of its quality advantages.

## Problem and motivation
Image-guided 3D texturing must preserve reference-image details while producing geometry-aligned views for projection onto a mesh. The authors identify computational cost as a barrier to increasing resolution: their dense six-view setup at 2048 resolution requires 212,992 tokens across targets, normal-map conditions, and the reference image (§3.1).

Their central observation is that object-centric renderings contain extensive empty background, while attention among foreground tokens is also sparse (§§3.2–3.3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Foreground computation:** Remove background tokens before the FLUX-based MM-DiT using downsampled, slightly dilated alpha masks; preserve original positional indices and supervise only foreground targets (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Sparse foreground attention:** Select key blocks using mean-pooled query–key scores, then compute token-level attention within selected blocks. The final model retains 20% of key blocks (§3.3; §4.1).
- **Adapted decoding:** Replace untouched background noise with an encoded black-image latent and fine-tune the VAE decoder using foreground reconstruction loss (§3.4).
- **Training data:** Construct G-buffer TexVerse from over 268,000 filtered assets, with geometry/material maps and shaded observations rendered up to 4096 × 4096. Training uses normal-map conditions and views resized to 2048 × 2048 (§3.5; Appendix A).

The contribution is the coordinated foreground-aware pipeline and dataset; FLUX, flow matching, and sparse attention are existing foundations adapted within it.

The text supports a concrete efficiency mechanism and reports substantial acceleration in sparse, object-centric settings. It does not provide the numerical table bodies needed to assess quality margins, absolute latency, memory savings, or reconstruction gains independently. The headline speedups concern the computational baseline, not the four competing texturing systems.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Repetitive textures remain difficult; VAE compression loses some high-frequency detail; further scaling beyond the foundation model’s operating range may require redesigned DiT–VAE models (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** Efficiency gains depend strongly on foreground occupancy. The dataset predominantly contains sparse foregrounds, limiting extrapolation to tightly framed or scene-level inputs (Appendices A–B).
- **Analyst inference:** The retention-ratio study substitutes sparse attention at lower-resolution inference; it does not establish the optimal quality–efficiency trade-off for separately trained high-resolution models (§4.6).
- **Analyst inference:** Main relighting comparisons use fixed roughness/metallic of 1/0 for UltraTex and non-PBR baselines, so they do not establish complete material recovery (§4.3). Appendix D adds only a qualitative extension.
- **Scope and resources:** Quantitative quality evaluation covers one held-out dataset; broader inputs are qualitative. Training requires 64 H200 GPUs over stages reported as 1, 8, and 9 days (§§4.1, 4.3–4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Filtering out simple albedo, nonstandard materials, and metadata-tagged AI assets narrows the demonstrated data distribution (Appendix A).

## Open research questions
1. **How robust is token dropping to mask errors?** Masks determine which tokens survive, but perturbation tests are not reported. Evaluate controlled mask erosion, dilation, and segmentation errors using boundary quality and texture fidelity.
2. **What retention ratio works best after high-resolution training?** Current selection relies on a lower-resolution inference study. Train matched high-resolution variants with different retention ratios and compare quality, latency, and memory by foreground occupancy.
3. **Can richer foundation models fix repetitive textures and identity drift?** These remain limitations, and FLUX.2-dev evidence is a single qualitative comparison. Compare backbones on a dedicated repeated-pattern and identity-detail test set.
4. **Does the PBR extension improve relighting quantitatively?** Appendix D shows material maps without aggregate results. Evaluate predicted material channels and rendered relighting against ground truth under matched lighting.
