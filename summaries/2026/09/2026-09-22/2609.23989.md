# ACLArena: Agent Continue Learning in Multi-stage Post-training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23989) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23989)


## One-sentence takeaway

On a fixed Qwen3-8B-Base curriculum, ACLArena shows substantial forgetting across post-training stages and improves capability retention using a replay-trained shared backbone with separately routed, RL-trained LoRA experts, without winning every benchmark (Tables 1–2; §6).

## Problem and motivation

Agents must acquire reasoning, tool use, and instruction following while retaining earlier capabilities. The authors identify a shortage of controlled comparisons for this process: industrial reports disclose limited training details, while environment replay and joint retraining can become expensive. ACLArena provides a common pipeline for studying forgetting, transfer, and practical consolidation strategies (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled testbed:** Train through Math → Search → E-commerce → Instruction Following, using GSPO reinforcement learning and initial supervised fine-tuning for agentic tool protocols; evaluate both trained and held-out domains (§3.1; Appendices A–B).
- **Diagnostics and consolidation comparison:** Examine parameter displacement and fixed-prefix token prediction changes, then compare offline self-distilled fine-tuning (SDFT), model merging, and multi-teacher mixed on-policy distillation (MMOPD). The proposed mixed objective adds truncated forward-KL supervision at high-entropy teacher positions to reverse-KL distillation (§§3–5).
- **Mixture of Low-Rank Experts (MLE):** Consolidate filtered specialist trajectories through SDFT, freeze that backbone, and train separate LoRA experts with domain-specific RL. Observable environment context selects the expert at inference (§6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Sequential training causes substantial forgetting | From Seq-Search to Seq-E-commerce, in-domain AIME26 avg@16 falls from **23.33 to 6.04**, and NQ reported score from **45.2 ± 0.4 to 14.6 ± 0.1**. | Table 1; §3.3.1 |
| MLE improves agentic performance over the final sequential checkpoint | In-domain NQ: **49.7 ± 0.2 vs. 33.5 ± 1.7**; τ³-Retail task success: **32.9 ± 2.2 vs. 29.6 ± 1.6**. Independent experts reach **49.9 ± 1.7** and **33.2 ± 1.1**, respectively. | Table 2; §5.1 |
| MLE leads the reported OOD search comparisons | Single-hop average over PopQA/TriviaQA: **57.7 ± 0.0**, versus SDFT **55.6 ± 0.1**; multi-hop average over 2Wiki/Bamboogle/HotpotQA/MuSiQue: **38.6 ± 0.1**, versus SDFT **38.0 ± 0.2**. The exact search scoring rule is not specified. | Tables 1–2; §5.1 |
| MLE does not dominate all alternatives | AIME26 avg@16 is **21.04**, below SDFT’s **22.29**; OOD IF-Bench score is **29.1 ± 0.2**, below Seq-Final’s **30.8 ± 0.2**. | Table 2 |
| Single-teacher recovery has a retention cost | Distilling Seq-Search into Seq-E-commerce raises NQ from **14.6 ± 0.1 to 48.4 ± 0.0**, but reduces τ³-Retail success from **34.0 ± 0.9 to 15.8 ± 0.6**. | Table 3 |
| Prediction changes concentrate at uncertain positions | On fixed GPQA prefixes generated by Seq-Math, Seq-IF preserves top-token predictions at **0.9968** of positions with entropy below **0.5 nats**, versus **0.6838** above that threshold. | Appendix H; Table 7 |

These results support improved capability integration in the tested setting, with remaining task trade-offs. Table 1 explicitly reports three-run means and standard deviations, although AIME entries lack deviations. Parameter geometry and token consistency provide descriptive evidence; they do not independently establish a causal explanation of forgetting.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Generalization across model families, curricula, and longer training sequences remains unclear. Routing with ambiguous context, changing environments, or multiple specializations within one episode is untested (§7).
- **Analyst inference—comparison confounds:** SDFT uses independently trained oracle trajectories, MMOPD uses sequential teachers, and MLE adds domain-specific RL. The supplied experiments do not isolate architectural benefits under matched teacher quality and training budgets (Table 5).
- **Analyst inference—mechanistic limits:** Fixed-prefix token agreement does not directly measure free-running behavioral retention. Near-orthogonal parameter updates alone do not prove harmful interference (Appendices G–H).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** §5.1 specifies SDFT rollout group size eight, while Appendix D specifies sixteen samples per prompt. Main merging uses four sequential checkpoints, whereas Appendix E also includes the base checkpoint. The relationship between those results is unclear.
- **Claims exceed some tabulated evidence:** §5.3.1 says single-teacher distillation largely preserves e-commerce capability despite the substantial retail decline in Table 3. §5.3.3’s claim that balanced mixing improves average performance lacks an explicit aggregate and is not uniformly supported by Table 4.

## Open research questions

- **Does MLE help under matched training budgets?** Additional specialist RL confounds attribution. Compare MLE with shared-backbone RL using identical trajectories, environment interactions, and optimization budgets.
- **Can routing handle mixed-domain episodes?** The authors explicitly leave this untested. Evaluate ambiguous tool contexts and mid-episode domain switches using context routing, learned routing, and expert composition.
- **Does retention persist as capabilities accumulate?** Evidence covers a fixed curriculum. Add new domains after freezing the backbone, vary training order, and track retention alongside adapter memory and latency.
- **Are uncertain tokens causal drivers of forgetting?** Current evidence is observational. Compare entropy-targeted preservation against random-token preservation under matched supervision budgets.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main performance patterns and method description, but causal interpretation and reproducibility are less secure.

Material missing information includes MLE’s LoRA rank and target modules, detailed routing rules, matched compute costs, memory and latency measurements, exact search and instruction-following scoring variants, complete training hyperparameters, and confirmed code/checkpoint releases. The rollout and merging discrepancies also remain unresolved.
