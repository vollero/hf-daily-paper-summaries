# Grounded Action Model: 3D Grounding as a Foundation for Robotics

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23863) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23863)


## One-sentence takeaway
GAM uses a frozen, promptable 3D grounding backbone to improve manipulation robustness and target relocation performance on the reported benchmarks, though unequal planner access and additional perception supervision limit attribution of all gains to its grounding foundation.

## Problem and motivation
Manipulation requires identifying relevant objects and locating their geometry precisely. The authors argue that language and video pretraining do not explicitly enforce this metric grounding, leaving robot demonstrations to establish the connection between perception and action. With limited demonstrations, policies may instead depend on scene appearance or fixed layouts that fail under perturbation (§I).

## Approach and contributions
- **Grounding-based policy architecture:** GAM adapts the existing WildDet3D backbone into a manipulation system. Language, points, and boxes resolve into a shared object representation; the backbone stays frozen during action-policy training (§III-A).
- **Selective visual and geometric observations:** Image tokens retain features around selected objects and the robot arm. Detection tokens encode object point clouds, position, extent, and orientation. A multi-stream transformer combines these with joint-state history and language conditioning to generate action chunks through flow matching (§III-B–C).
- **Unified target-selection interface:** Tracking maintains object associations during execution. Humans or a Molmo2 planner can update targets through visual prompts; the planner supplies history-dependent selections for sequential tasks (§III-D).

The contribution is this integration and its evaluation; the grounding backbone, tracker, and planner are existing components.

The results support robustness and spatial responsiveness in the tested settings. Controlled ablations support the value of observation filtering and complementary streams within GAM. They do not independently establish that grounding pretraining is superior to other pretraining objectives: the backbone is unchanged across those ablations. Likewise, the authors’ interpretation that baseline failures reflect trajectory memorization is plausible but not uniquely demonstrated by success rates.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Grounding errors propagate into actions without recovery; filtering may omit relevant unselected obstacles. The authors propose richer grounding, failure recovery, and adaptive context selection (§V).
- **Author-stated:** Unseen object sizes may require grasp adjustments the policy cannot reliably make, despite receiving geometric information (§IV-B).
- **Analyst inference—comparison controls:** RoboTwin comparisons mix single-task and co-trained policies, and GAM receives separate simulator-derived perception supervision. Its adaptation data volume and coverage are unspecified (§IV-A).
- **Analyst inference—system attribution:** Franka gains combine policy, planner, and memory access; YAM comparisons use different target interfaces. Neither isolates the action model under matched interfaces and planning support (§IV-C).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Real-robot evidence covers one YAM task and four Franka tasks. LIBERO-PRO Task reassignment uses objects and actions already present in training, so it does not establish generalization to unseen manipulation skills (§IV-B–C).

## Open research questions
1. **How much benefit comes specifically from grounding pretraining?** Existing ablations hold the backbone fixed. Compare differently pretrained backbones with matched observation filtering, action heads, and supervision budgets.
2. **Can context selection preserve robustness while avoiding missed obstacles?** The authors identify omitted context as a limitation. Test fixed object filtering against adaptive obstacle inclusion in scenes with controlled obstacle placement.
3. **How much of the Franka improvement depends on planner access?** Baselines lack the external planner. Evaluate all controllers with matched target selections, episode history, and planner support.
4. **Can GAM handle substantial object-size changes?** Obj perturbations remain a weakness. Evaluate graded size shifts while separately varying geometric accuracy and size diversity in action demonstrations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly specifies the architecture and aggregate results, but causal and broader generalization claims have weaker support.

Material missing information includes confidence intervals and seed variability; LIBERO-PRO rollout counts; optimizer and training schedules; action horizon and inference-step settings; latency and compute; grounding-adaptation data coverage; quantitative grounding accuracy; matched-planner comparisons; and confirmation of released code, weights, or data.
