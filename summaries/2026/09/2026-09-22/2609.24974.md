# Harness-Zero: Harness Distillation via Agent-as-Harness

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.24974) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24974)


## One-sentence takeaway

Harness-Zero uses harness-guided response corrections to train Qwen3.5-9B under a fixed target harness, raising reported macro-average performance across three separately trained domains from 23.3% to 44.3% after removing the specialized harnesses (Table 2; Appendix E.3).

## Problem and motivation

Optimized agent harnesses supply tools, workflows, memory, and execution controls, but their benefits usually require retaining that infrastructure at deployment. The authors identify a transfer problem: demonstrations collected under a specialized harness may depend on actions or information unavailable in the deployment harness, making direct imitation ineffective (§§1–3).

The goal is to move useful harness-induced behavior into model weights while keeping a fixed deployment interface.

## Approach and contributions

- **Harness distillation formulation:** Evolve a domain-specific student harness, then adapt its tools into action recipes, middleware into private review checks, and skills and memory into intervention guidance (§3.1; Table 6).
- **Agent-as-harness supervision:** A reviewer intercepts student responses and either passes them or supplies a minimally changed, complete replacement executable through the target harness. The reviewer sees the student’s visible trajectory and private harness guidance, but cannot independently inspect the student sandbox or hidden solutions (§3.2).
- **Training and removal:** Apply supervised fine-tuning to accepted trajectories, masking detected reviewer-perspective reasoning. Deployment retains the trained student and target harness only (§3.3; Appendix D).

The experiments use LoRA training separately for each domain. They do **not** demonstrate one model jointly absorbing all three harnesses (Appendix E.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Training-free review improves average performance | Across two frontier models and three benchmarks, evolved-guidance review averages **81.1%**, versus **78.1%** with the evolved code harness, **68.6%** with the minimal harness, and **69.2%** with unguided review. These averages combine spreadsheet/USPTO pass@1 and AppWorld SGC. | Table 1; §§4.1–4.2 |
| Distillation improves the domain macro average | Qwen3.5-9B with Harness-Zero reaches **44.3%**, versus **23.3%** under the minimal harness and **41.7%** with the evolved harness attached; domain checkpoints are trained separately. | Table 2; Appendix E.3 |
| Retention varies by domain | Distilled / minimal-harness base / evolved-harness base: SpreadsheetBench pass@1 **44.0 / 31.0 / 39.0%**; AppWorld SGC **58.9 / 26.8 / 48.2%**; USPTO pass@1 **30.0 / 12.0 / 38.0%**. | Table 2 |
| Harness-guided corrections outperform tested supervision alternatives | On USPTO, using the same 500 collection tasks and two-epoch training recipe, distilled test pass@1 is **30.0%** for Harness-Zero; **12.0%** for teacher rollouts; **3.0%** for teacher-with-harness rollouts; **12.0%** for student-with-harness rollouts; **11.0%** for empty-guidance review; **15.0%** for oracle-answer review. | Table 3; §4.4 |

| Selected behaviors recur after distillation | Authors report **82.3%** average recovery across **28** patterns, each evaluated on at least **10** test tasks where the base model exhibited it only with the evolved harness. | Table 4; §4.5; Appendix G |

Reported mean USPTO trial latency is **237.2 s** with guided review versus **100.1 s** with the minimal harness; the latency comparison’s model is not specified there. | Table 8; Appendix F |

These results support transfer of useful behavior into domain-specific student checkpoints under the evaluated target harness. They do not establish universal superiority over code harnesses, complete retention of domain knowledge, or transfer into a single general-purpose model. Behavior recovery measures detector matches on selected task subsets, rather than causal contributions to task success.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Weak reviewers can harm performance; reviewing every proposal adds collection cost; deep domain knowledge is incompletely retained; some mechanisms, including context management, are not readily expressible as student responses (§5; Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization evidence:** Distillation uses one student model family, separate domain training, and one target interface. Cross-harness deployment and joint multidomain internalization remain untested (§4.1; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and causal attribution:** No confidence intervals or repeated-seed results are supplied. Alternative-supervision ablations cover USPTO only, and do not separately isolate every adaptation, filtering, or minimal-correction design choice (§4.4; Appendix E.3).
- **Analyst inference—behavior measurement:** Patterns and support tasks are selected using harness-versus-base differences on test trajectories; base recovery is zero by construction. Detector validity is not independently quantified (§4.5; Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference—comparison controls:** Frontier review adds inference computation, while base and fine-tuned Qwen evaluations use different serving routes. Compute-matched review comparisons and fully matched serving configurations are not established (§4.2; Appendix E.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Can one checkpoint absorb multiple domain harnesses without interference?** Training is domain-specific here. Next experiment: compare joint training against separate checkpoints at matched training budgets, including held-out domains.
2. **Can selective review preserve gains while reducing collection cost?** Every proposal is currently reviewed. Next experiment: compare uncertainty-triggered review with full review at matched intervention budgets, measuring distilled accuracy, tokens, and latency.
3. **What closes the retrosynthesis retention gap?** The distilled model trails the attached harness on USPTO. Next experiment: vary trajectory volume and separately ablate chemical priors, candidate-generation recipes, and validation guidance.
4. **Do recovered behaviors cause better outcomes?** Detector recovery establishes occurrence, not necessity. Next experiment: selectively remove procedural guidance during collection and measure changes in both behavior recovery and task success.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, prompts, training settings, and benchmark results, but supports narrower conclusions than universal harness internalization.

Material missing information includes repeated-run uncertainty, compute-matched comparisons, independent detector validation, joint multidomain results, cross-target-harness transfer, exact provider decoding settings, and verified availability of code, checkpoints, and collected trajectories.
