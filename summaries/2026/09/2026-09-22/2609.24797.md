# Complex KDA: Understanding and Enhancing the Expressivity of Kimi Delta Attention

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.24797) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24797)


## One-sentence takeaway
CKDA combines signed channel-wise gates with an extended delta-rule coefficient to enable rotations within KDA’s existing transition structure, improving synthetic state-tracking extrapolation while largely matching KDA’s language-modeling performance (§§3–6).

## Problem and motivation
Linear RNNs provide efficient sequence processing, but their restricted transition matrices limit which evolving states they can represent. Standard KDA’s nonnegative channel-wise gates permit only real eigenvalues; DeltaProduct can obtain rotations by composing additional delta-rule updates. The authors investigate whether KDA’s channel-wise gate can supply the missing transformation without increasing the rank of its correction (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Transition design:** CKDA uses \(A=(I-\beta kk^\top)\operatorname{Diag}(\alpha)\), with unit keys, \(\alpha\in[-1,1]^n\), and \(\beta\in[0,2]\). A signed coordinate reflection and a Householder reflection jointly implement planar rotations. The recurrence remains real-valued, diagonal-plus-rank-one, and non-expansive. The contribution combines two existing range extensions (§3).
- **Expressivity characterization:** The authors characterize all orthogonal diagonal-plus-rank-one matrices as CKDA transitions, establish spectral and product-factorization bounds, and give finite-group tracking constructions and a conditional impossibility result (§§4–5; Appendix A). They acknowledge that the orthogonal characterization can also be derived from an earlier mathematical result (Appendix A.1).
- **Implementation and evaluation:** Absorbing cumulative gate signs into keys and queries enables reuse of KDA kernels. Experiments examine range settings, initialization, state tracking, periodic continuation, language modeling, and scaling (§6; Appendices E–H).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Rotations without a higher-rank correction | Every orthogonal diagonal-plus-rank-one matrix has CKDA form; each CKDA transition permits at most one non-real conjugate eigenvalue pair. | Theorems 1, 8; Appendices A.1, A.3 |
| Stronger tracking constructions, with explicit limits | One layer tracks every finite group isomorphic to a subgroup of SO(3). One-layer S₅ tracking is excluded under non-expansion and finite reachability, even with finitely many independent heads. Three layers suffice for arbitrary finite-group word problems under exact arithmetic. | Theorems 3–5; Appendix B.4 |
| Best tested KDA range setting for group extrapolation | With training lengths up to 32 and best-of-three-seed reporting, CKDA extrapolates best on S₃ and S₄; S₄ accuracy still deteriorates at longer lengths. Standard training fails on A₅; a separately configured theory-initialized model succeeds. | §6; Figure 6; Appendix G.2 |
| Periodic continuation improves markedly | At sequence length 264, beyond maximum training length 136, CKDA achieves 38.1 dB SNR versus 2.8 dB for the causal Transformer. Evaluation averages continuation error over all phases of one fixed groove. GRU has lower error than CKDA. | §6; Figure 8; Appendix G.3 |
| Large language models approximately match KDA | At 1.3B parameters and 100B FineWeb-Edu tokens, recurrent CKDA averages 54.06% downstream accuracy versus 54.09% for the authors’ bounded-gate KDA; LAMBADA perplexity is 10.08 versus 10.53. | Table 2 |
| Small-model improvement depends on configuration | At approximately 340M parameters and 15B Nemotron-CC tokens, spread-initialized CKDA averages 52.30% accuracy versus 51.32% for standard KDA, but validation perplexity is 17.49 versus KDA’s better 17.45. | §6; Tables 4, 6 |
| Scaling favors both recurrent architectures over the tested Transformer | At the 1.7B/50B-token Nemotron-CC endpoint, validation loss is 2.1061 nats for CKDA, 2.1046 for KDA, and 2.1374 for the QK-normalized Transformer. Authors conclude CKDA and KDA perform on par given uncertainty. | Appendix H.2; Tables 11–12 |
| Near-KDA recurrence throughput | Fused implementations retain approximately 96–97% of KDA throughput on H100 with BF16 inputs, 16 heads, key/value dimensions 128, and 32,768 logical tokens per step. Projections and optimizer updates are excluded. | Figure 5; Appendix E |

The evidence supports additional representational capacity and better extrapolation on the tested synthetic tasks. It does **not** establish reliable discovery of every representable solution, consistent language-model superiority over matched KDA, or an inherent efficiency advantage over DeltaProduct 2. Learned complex transitions support the proposed mechanism’s presence, but their functional role in language modeling remains unresolved (§§6–7).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated theoretical scope:** Exact constructions do not establish floating-point robustness at arbitrary lengths. The S₅ lower bound requires finite exact reachability; general weighted-automaton simulation permits \(\beta>2\), losing guaranteed non-expansiveness (Appendix B.4; §7).
- **Author-stated learning gap:** A₅ is representable but not learned under standard training. The successful initialized experiment also changes architecture and schedule, preventing attribution to initialization alone (§6).
- **Analyst inference—limited empirical robustness:** Best-of-three reporting for group tasks and a single training seed on one fixed audio groove limit conclusions about typical training outcomes and broader audio generalization (Appendices G.2–G.3).
- **Comparison constraints:** Published hybrid baselines use different attention ratios and attention types. Scaling reuses hyperparameters selected for another backbone; bootstrap intervals exclude that systematic uncertainty (§6; Appendix H.1).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Table 7’s caption says hybrid retrieval evaluations stop at the training context, yet its hybrid rows contain entries under longer-context columns. The authors also warn that retrieval scores are prompt-sensitive (Appendix G.4; Table 7).
- **Incomplete source:** The supplied text contains a collector-truncated middle passage, preventing complete assessment of the proofs and supporting discussion.

## Open research questions
1. **Can ordinary training reliably discover A₅ tracking?** The current initialization comparison changes several variables. A matched experiment varying only initialization, repeated across seeds, would isolate its effect (§6).
2. **Do learned rotations causally improve language-model behavior?** Complex transitions emerge, but their roles remain unclear. Layer-specific gate-sign and coefficient interventions could measure effects on perplexity and tracking-sensitive evaluations (§6; Figure 10).
3. **How long do tracking solutions remain accurate under practical arithmetic?** Exact constructions provide no accumulated-error guarantee. Precision sweeps and controlled perturbations over increasing sequence lengths could measure failure horizons (Appendix B.4).
4. **Are two CKDA layers sufficient for arbitrary finite-group tracking?** The one-layer obstruction and three-layer construction leave this undecided. Two-layer S₅ experiments with controlled decoders could guide a construction or stronger lower bound (Appendix D.5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central mechanism and reported comparisons, but coverage is incomplete. Missing or unresolved information includes the omitted proof passage, full seed distributions for group results, repeated-run uncertainty for fixed-scale language evaluations, a model-release location, and clarification of Table 7’s context-length inconsistency.
