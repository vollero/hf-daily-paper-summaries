# Document Retrieval-Aware Chunking (D-RAC): Universal Retrieval-Aware Ingestion of Enterprise Documents via PDF Normalization and Multimodal Markdown Conversion

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.24220) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24220)


## One-sentence takeaway
D-RAC combines multimodal PDF-to-Markdown conversion with ID-based chunk planning, reporting better retrieval than fixed-size PDF extraction and substantially cheaper planning than agentic chunking, while leaving conversion fidelity and non-PDF generalization unverified.

## Problem and motivation
Enterprise documents contain tables, columns, and visual heading hierarchies that rule-based extraction can damage, weakening retrieval. The authors’ earlier W-RAC framework plans chunks over structured element identifiers, but depends on recoverable document structure. D-RAC addresses this prerequisite by converting rendered pages into structured, retrieval-oriented Markdown before planning (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Document ingestion extension:** Normalize inputs to PDF, render pages, and use a multimodal LLM to recover Markdown structure. This is the main extension to W-RAC; ID-based planning and verbatim reconstruction are inherited (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Retrieval-oriented conversion:** Rewrite each table row as a self-contained sentence with column context, preserve heading hierarchy, add page provenance, and suppress images—including charts (§3.2.2; §4).
- **Large-document sectioning:** Recursively split at heading boundaries within a planning budget, carrying ancestor headings into each section. The planner emits element-ID arrays; local reconstruction preserves converted text, and fallback grouping covers omitted content IDs (§§3.2.3–3.2.4).

These results support operational feasibility and improved retrieval under the stated benchmark protocol. They do not isolate which pipeline component causes the gains, establish end-to-end cost savings including conversion, or verify faithful preservation of source facts. Many numerical table bodies are missing from the supplied text; the results above come from prose and captions.

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper provides no dedicated limitations discussion. Its stated scope and analyst-inferred risks are:

- **Format coverage:** All evaluated corpus inputs were native PDFs, making normalization an identity operation (§5). **Analyst inference:** Claims covering office files and scans remain experimentally untested here.
- **Conversion fidelity:** “Zero errors” and complete element coverage are reported, but no source-to-Markdown factual audit is supplied. Charts are explicitly omitted (§§3.2.2, 6.1–6.2). **Analyst inference:** Lossless reconstruction applies to converted text, not necessarily to source information.
- **Retrieval validity:** Relevance requires at least 60% of a supporting fact’s content words to appear in a chunk (§6.3). **Analyst inference:** This lexical judge may treat paraphrases and semantically incorrect overlaps differently from human judgments; identical scoring alone does not resolve that risk.
- **Confounded comparisons:** Fixed-size extraction and D-RAC differ in both conversion and chunking, while agentic reference chunks come from clean structured sources (§6.3). No component ablations are supplied, so attribution to table prose or heading recovery remains uncertain.
- **Cost precision and comparability:** Despite describing counts as exact, the paper converts characters to tokens using a fixed ratio. Frontier-model prices are applied to artifacts, while measured D-RAC planning uses Gemma-3 27B; baseline latency comes from prior experiments (§§6.2–6.4).
- **Generalization:** The corpus represents fictional enterprises, and scalability evidence includes one long prospectus (§5). **Analyst inference:** This demonstrates a long-document run, but does not establish a general linear scaling law.

## Open research questions
- **Which components improve retrieval?** No ablation separates conversion, table prose, hierarchy, and planning. Next experiment: hold converted content and embeddings constant while independently varying these components.
- **How faithfully does conversion preserve facts?** Operational success does not measure omissions, altered values, or chart information. Next experiment: audit source-to-Markdown fidelity on annotated tables, charts, and scanned pages.
- **Does the pipeline generalize beyond native PDFs?** Normalization was not exercised in the benchmark. Next experiment: evaluate matched DOCX, PPTX, XLSX, scan, and PDF versions for conversion fidelity and retrieval.
- **When do total costs favor D-RAC?** Reported savings exclude initial conversion. Next experiment: measure complete ingestion and repeated re-chunking costs against baselines under matched models, concurrency, and source inputs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The pipeline and headline retrieval results are clearly described, but broader fidelity, cost, and universality claims have weaker support.

Material missing information includes table bodies, actual conversion and planning prompts despite appendix headings, conversion-fidelity measurements, component ablations, statistical uncertainty, full agentic baseline configuration, end-to-end monetary costs, and a confirmed D-RAC implementation release.
