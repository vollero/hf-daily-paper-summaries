# One to More, More to One: Category-Aware Iterative Expert Training for Software Engineering Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23377) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23377)


## One-sentence takeaway
Category-specific expert training followed by single-model distillation improves reported software-issue resolution over pooled and balanced RL, but benchmark-based checkpoint selection and missing component ablations limit causal conclusions (Tables 12–14; §8).

## Problem and motivation
Repository-level software engineering combines tasks with different tool interactions, verification needs, and learning dynamics. The authors observe a “category see-saw”: aggregate RL gains can conceal regressions in individual task categories. Balancing category exposure does not eliminate these opposing movements, motivating separate expert development followed by integration into one deployable agent (§§3.2, 6.1).

The identified gap is specialization **within** software engineering using observable task categories, rather than decomposition into workflow stages or conventional broad domains (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Category construction:** SWE Labeler assigns hierarchical task-type and repository-domain labels plus modification scope, cognitive complexity, and estimated time. A deterministic domain mapping produces service/data/security, user-facing application, and systems/tooling/runtime routes. Its structural audit establishes schema consistency, not held-out semantic accuracy (§4.2; Appendix B).
- **Iterative expert development:** Same-origin experts alternate Agentic-miniRL with Refresh–Repair–Expand: remeasure task mastery, supervise on their own verified successful trajectories, and select a refreshed task pool. Agentic-miniRL combines existing optimization ingredients for long-horizon tool use (§§4.3–4.4).
- **Single-policy integration:** Label-routed multi-teacher on-policy distillation trains a base-initialized student on its own trajectories. ReLU-gated teacher–reference extrapolation supplements imitation; executable rewards do not enter the distillation loss (§4.5).

The authors claim novelty in this within-SWE organization and iterative framework, while explicitly building on prior MiniRL, MOPD, and extrapolation methods. “No external trajectory teacher” applies to solution supervision; external models still support task construction and labeling (§§4.1, 5.5).

The evidence supports higher observed aggregate and routed-category means for the complete framework. It does not establish which component causes the gains or superiority under equal total training compute. ## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ limitations:** Hard routing simplifies overlapping tasks; expert training and teacher inference add substantial compute; optimization components, replay choices, and routing granularity are not isolated (§8).
- **Analyst inference—selection bias:** Pro-618 guides checkpoint selection and supplies the main reported results. Those scores are therefore not an untouched test estimate; repeated evaluation does not remove this dependence (§§5.3, 5.8).
- **Authors’ diagnostic caveats:** Training-instance mastery estimates usually use few rollouts, include repeated records, and are vulnerable to sampling noise and regression to the mean. Recovery snapshots lack a no-SFT causal control (Appendix D).
- **Scope and measurement:** Evaluation uses one base-model family and two policy benchmarks. Pro-618 results cannot establish full-Pro performance; executable tests do not fully measure patch quality or eliminate memorization and verifier weaknesses (§§5.2–5.4, 8).
- **Label validity:** The inventory was finalized using the audited benchmark collection; structural consistency is not evidence of independent semantic annotation accuracy (Appendix B.2).

## Open research questions
- **Does specialization outperform equally funded joint training?** Total-compute superiority is unestablished. Compare RRE plus integration against joint RL given equal rollout, refresh, SFT, and distillation budgets.
- **Which components produce the gains?** Stage-wise results do not isolate mechanisms. Run matched-budget ablations of mastery-weighted replay, expansion, and gated extrapolation with separate validation tasks.
- **Would overlapping routes improve transfer?** Hard routing cannot represent mixed-category tasks. Compare hard routing with multi-label teacher mixtures and audit label accuracy independently.
- **Do gains persist on untouched repositories?** Pro-618 participates in selection. Freeze the recipe before evaluating newly held-out repositories, reporting executable success and independent patch-quality judgments.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods and repeated evaluation results, but causal attribution and generalization remain limited.

Material missing information includes component ablations, matched total-compute comparisons, independent training-seed replication, held-out label accuracy, an explicit final MOPD checkpoint-selection rule, and directly inspectable release artifacts and manifests.
