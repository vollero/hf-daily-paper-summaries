# IntBMoE: Integrating Block-Level Conditioning into Expert Composition for Full-Participation Mixture-of-Experts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.21346) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21346)


## One-sentence takeaway
IntBMoE composes a fixed set of reusable blocks from all expert bases and sparsely routes tokens through them, improving reported vision, language, and recommendation metrics while making cached inference cost independent of expert-pool size under fixed architectural settings (§§4–6).

## Problem and motivation
The authors distinguish **participation**—which experts contribute to an output—from **execution**—which transformations run—and **materialization**—which composed parameter sets must be constructed. Sparse routing limits participation, dense output mixing increases execution, and input-conditioned parameter merging requires compositions for different routing units. Their proposed gap is an architecture combining full-pool composition, sparse execution, and a bounded number of reusable compositions (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Codebook-conditioned composition:** A hypernetwork maps learned, input-independent block embeddings to signed, unnormalized coefficients. These combine layer-specific expert bases into reusable blocks; each block shares its composition recipe across internal layers (§4.2).
- **Sparse block execution and caching:** Each token selects blocks through Top-*k* routing, receives block-conditioned feature filtering, and combines routed outputs with an always-active shared SwiGLU expert. Fixed inference parameters allow compositions to be cached (§§4.3–4.7).
- **Dual-Path Residual Gating:** Separately composed value and gate paths interact multiplicatively through a learnable residual scale, adding nonlinear interactions without increasing the expert-basis pool (§4.5).

The central architectural contribution is separating input-independent parameter composition from token-dependent block selection.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

| Cached costs remain constant in the measured sweep | ImageNet, batch size 1, expert count 1–128: cached inference remains at 104.33 MB and 3.457 GFLOPs; uncached measurements rise from 54.57 to 627.83 MB and 3.495 to 8.305 GFLOPs. | §5.6; Fig. 7 |

| Authors report production benefit | One-week AMap A/B test against the existing model without MoE, approximately 5,000 queries/s: 2.4% relative UVCTR improvement, 19 ms average latency, and 38 ms P99 within a 60 ms budget on T-Head PPUs. | §6, pp. 13–14 |

These results support effectiveness in the tested settings and the benefit of caching input-independent compositions. They do **not** establish equal-compute superiority: ImageNet comparisons primarily match parameter budgets. Basis-removal results show aggregate checkpoint dependence, rather than proving every basis meaningfully benefits every token.

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper has no dedicated limitations discussion. It explicitly acknowledges composition cost remaining linear in expert count before caching, and caching’s higher memory cost for small pools (§§4.7, 5.6).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the reported evaluation:**

- **Compute confounding:** Higher ImageNet accuracy accompanies higher FLOPs than several baselines; an equal-compute comparison is absent (Table 1).
- **Statistical uncertainty:** Three seeds are specified, but tables provide no dispersion or confidence intervals. Online sample sizes, allocation, and significance estimates are absent (Appendix A.1; §6).
- **Limited scaling evidence:** Language evaluation uses one MiniPile training epoch and the specified causal backbone; larger-scale language training and downstream language tasks are not evaluated (Appendix A.2).
- **Participation interpretation:** Unconstrained coefficients can differ greatly in influence. Removal tests support usefulness of examined bases but do not demonstrate uniform contributions (§§4.2, 5.4).
- **Systems accounting:** Cached memory and FLOPs are reported, but cache-build latency, training throughput, and detailed accounting of retained source parameters are not supplied (§5.6).

## Open research questions
- **Does the advantage persist at equal compute?** Parameter matching leaves execution costs unequal. Compare tuned baselines and IntBMoE at matched training compute and inference FLOPs.
- **How restrictive is the fixed composition codebook?** Increasing block count yields diminishing ImageNet gains, but broader task diversity is untested. Sweep codebook size on more varied causal tasks while measuring quality and memory.
- **Are all bases necessary after adaptation?** Removal tests disable bases without retraining. Prune low-impact bases, retrain, and compare accuracy and composition cost.
- **How does caching behave during model refreshes?** Reported serving results omit rebuild overhead. Measure cache construction, refresh frequency, and latency during production-style updates.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives clear equations, configurations, and comparative results, but lacks uncertainty estimates, equal-compute evaluations, training-efficiency measurements, detailed cache accounting, and online experiment statistics. Code availability is author-reported; repository contents and reproducibility were not verified.
