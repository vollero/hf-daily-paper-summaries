# From Pretraining to Proficiency: Real-World Subtask RL for Long-Horizon Manipulation with Minimal Human Intervention

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.21788) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21788)


## One-sentence takeaway
PARTS improves a frozen robot policy through locally rewarded residual learning at human-selected bottlenecks, raising earbud full-task success from 10% to 40% and cable success from 50% to 95%, while retaining task-dependent human supervision (Fig. 4; Table I).

## Problem and motivation
Long-horizon manipulation can fail because of a few unreliable subtasks, even when a pretrained policy performs the remaining behaviors well. Full-task demonstrations repeat already reliable actions, while terminal-reward RL provides little positive feedback and rarely reaches later bottlenecks after early failures.

The authors target efficient local practice that transfers back into complete-task execution, including preparatory behaviors whose outcomes determine whether subsequent precision operations succeed (§§I, III-A).

## Approach and contributions
- **Targeted policy adaptation:** After task-specific supervised fine-tuning, PARTS freezes the π0.5 base policy and learns separate bounded residual policies for selected bottlenecks using chunk-level TD3+BC and binary local rewards. Human-defined masks restrict which action coordinates each residual can change (§III-B).
- **Executable subtask supervision:** Humans specify entry conditions, outcome criteria, correction bounds, and motion budgets. Coding agents implement selectors, verifiers, and reset programs using visual perception; humans review their decisions. These programs coordinate repeated transitions between base and residual policies (§III-C).
- **Success-reweighted retraining and redeployment:** Fresh residual networks train on all successful episodes plus a sampled fraction of failures. An operator selects a checkpoint, which resumes online learning and collects further experience (§III-D).

The contribution is principally this integrated framework for bottleneck adaptation; the paper explicitly builds on existing residual RL, TD3+BC, and offline retraining methods.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Earbud task improves | Over 20 full-task evaluation episodes per method, binary success: SFT 10%, DSRL 15%, EXPO-FT 0%, RLT 0%, PARTS 40%; RL methods receive matched task-specific rollout time. | Fig. 4; §§IV-A–B |
| Cable task improves | Over 20 episodes per method, binary success: SFT 50%, DSRL 65%, EXPO-FT 20%, RLT 70%, PARTS 95%. PARTS uses 29 minutes of RL rollouts. | Fig. 4; §IV-C, Q1 |
| LEGO progress improves | Mean fraction of ten bricks sorted: SFT 54%, DSRL 44.5%, EXPO-FT 48%, RLT 50%, PARTS 82%; 20 episodes per method. PARTS uses 17 minutes of RL rollouts. | Fig. 4; §§IV-A, IV-C |
| Retraining helps on earbuds | Removing retraining for insertion residuals reduces binary full-task success from 40% to 25%; ablation evaluated on earbuds only. | Fig. 4; §IV-C, Q3 |
| Human involvement remains task-dependent | LEGO and cable use automatic reward labels; humans check earbud verifier labels and perform resets when necessary. | Table I; §IV-A |

These results support improved performance for the complete PARTS system under matched **active robot-rollout time**, which excludes resets and update pauses. They do not establish equal total time, compute, or human effort.

The abstract’s YAM “full-task success” increase from 32% to 61% needs qualification: **analyst inference**, those numbers equal averages of earbud binary success and LEGO progress, which are different metrics. Fig. 4 reports no LEGO binary full-task success.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged supervision:** Humans identify bottlenecks, define contracts, review generated programs, select retrained checkpoints, and sometimes label or reset attempts. Broad claims of label-free training have an explicit earbud exception (§§III-C–D, IV-A; Table I).
- **Author-acknowledged distribution mismatch:** Restaged local practice can differ from states reached during full-task execution, encouraging overfitting (§III-D).
- **Analyst inference—limited precision and scope:** Evaluation covers three tasks, one backbone, and 20 episodes per method per task. Training-seed variability and confidence intervals are not reported.
- **Analyst inference—component attribution remains unclear:** The earbud ablation removes the retraining package; it does not separately isolate success reweighting, fresh initialization, or redeployment. Local rewards, switching, and reset strategies also lack separate ablations.
- **Comparison boundaries:** Baselines use modified supervision regimes; RLT is reproduced without official code and becomes full-task RL on LEGO. Results therefore apply to these implementations and budgets (§IV-B).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** LEGO’s stated deadline is 150 seconds, while later prose calls it a 120-second task; the explanation for omitting its binary metric is also unclear (§§IV-A, IV-C).

## Open research questions
- **How robust is local learning to entry-state variation?** The paper identifies restaging mismatch without quantifying it. Next experiment: compare fixed restaging against varied, full-task-generated entry states.
- **Which retraining component produces the gain?** The current ablation combines several changes. Next experiment: independently vary success reweighting, network reinitialization, and redeployment under matched budgets.
- **How much human effort does PARTS actually save?** Supervision categories are reported, but labor is not measured. Next experiment: record setup, labeling, reset, and checkpoint-selection time alongside total elapsed training time.
- **Can automatic verification handle ambiguous contact outcomes?** Earbud labeling still requires human checking. Next experiment: measure verifier errors against independent labels and evaluate their effect on learning and handoffs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and task-level results are explicit, but aggregate metric wording and limited evaluation constrain broader conclusions.

Missing material information includes the earbud rollout budget, exact demonstration counts, key training hyperparameters and retraining schedule, checkpoint-selection criteria, verifier accuracy, human labor measurements, repeated-training uncertainty, and confirmed availability of code, models, or datasets.
