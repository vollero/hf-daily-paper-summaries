# Designer-RSI: Evolving Procedural Memory from User Traffic for Agentic Graphic Design

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.22086) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22086)


## One-sentence takeaway
Evolving a frozen graphic-design agent’s procedural memory improves automated measures of task completion, with combined skill acquisition and revision outperforming either alone, although gains vary across models and benchmarks (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
Professional graphic design requires long sequences of interdependent operations that produce editable artifacts. Feedback mixes concrete requirements with subjective visual judgments, making failures difficult to attribute and preventing reliable automated success verification. The authors address how an agent can improve from user traffic without updating model weights or collecting human reward labels (§1).

Their identified gap is a shared procedural-memory system that both acquires and repairs workflows while screening updates for regressions under noisy feedback (§1; Appendix A).

## Approach and contributions
- **Coupled skill acquisition and revision.** A frozen agent operates more than 230 design tools. Widening distills recurring uncovered subtasks into new natural-language skills; deepening revises failure-associated skills using their successful and unsuccessful executions (§§2–3).
- **Matched replay admission.** Proposed skills are compared with the incumbent skill—or no skill for new additions—under matched upstream contexts and balanced presentation orders. Admission requires an observed improvement without an observed regression on replay cases (§3.3; Appendix F).
- **Deployment-oriented procedural memory.** Versioned Markdown skills contain workflows, prerequisites, tool references, and error handling. Operation-based retrieval ranks a catalog; the agent chooses which full skills to load. Optional tool-list narrowing is **off by default in experiments** (Appendix C).
- **Evidence for coupling.** The principal empirical contribution is that acquiring and revising procedures together improves completeness more than either isolated bank variant (§5). Personalization examples are exploratory and excluded from the main experiments (Appendix I).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Memory grows through automated evolution | Five rounds over 1,406 briefs and 1,869 graded trajectories grow the bank from 76 to 139 skills, without weight updates or human reward labels. | §4.1 |
| Sonnet improves substantially on GenEval2 | On 300 sampled prompts, BASE→EVOLVE execution success rises 72.7%→99.3%; Soft-TIFA geometric-mean quality rises 34.26→46.25, evaluated over successful generations. | §4; Table 1 |
| Specialized-design preferences favor EVOLVE overall | GPT-5.4 blind, two-order comparisons across four benchmarks, sampling 300 prompts each: overall win rates versus BASE are 67.6% for Opus, 61.8% for Sonnet, and 62.8% for Qwen. | §4; Table 2 |
| Combined mechanisms outperform isolated variants | On 200 unseen internal briefs, completeness is 68.62 for cold start, 69.02 for rewrite-only, 69.79 for new-skills-only, and 74.04 for EVOLVE. Corresponding win rates against BASE are 46.4%, 48.6%, 49.4%, and 58.5%; authors report p=0.025 for EVOLVE versus BASE. | §5; Table 3 |
| Improvements concentrate in completeness | On the same ablation set, BASE→EVOLVE aesthetics changes only 65.92→66.53; authors describe critique as largely unchanged. | §5; Table 3 |
| Important regressions remain | Opus GenEval2 quality falls 61.24→57.92. Qwen DPG-Bench success falls 44.0%→28.7%, and average specialized-design completion falls 65.5%→63.2%; authors attribute Qwen fluctuations largely to deployment instability. | Tables 1, 4; Appendix H |

These results support benefits for this agent’s automated task-completion measures and a useful interaction between acquisition and revision. They do not establish human preference gains, universal reliability improvement, or superiority over other memory-evolution methods. Quality scores conditioned on successful outputs also prevent a straightforward comparison of overall capability across backbones (§4.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Prose skills may fail to override default model strategies; geometric precision and long-procedure adherence remain unreliable. Replay protection applies only to evaluated cases, and intermediate rounds can regress (§6; Appendix I).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation dependence:** Graphic-Eval drives evolution and measures internal progress. Without human validation, improvements could partly reflect alignment with that judge’s criteria; a human study is only planned (Appendices D, K).
- **Analyst inference—limited causal isolation:** No gate-removal ablation is reported. The widening/deepening ablation uses intermediate bank variants, so it does not fully isolate independently evolved processes (§5).
- **Reproducibility and specification:** User-derived prompts and trajectories are withheld. Appendix F gives no numerical tie-band width or complete rollout budget; §5 describes top-three matching while Appendix C describes two-to-four ranked matches.

## Open research questions
1. **Do gains transfer to human judgments?** Automated evaluation dominates, and the human study is pending. Next experiment: blinded designer comparisons measuring brief satisfaction, aesthetics, and editability (Appendix K).
2. **How much does the replay gate prevent broader regressions?** Its protection is local, and intermediate-round regressions remain. Next experiment: compare matched gating, ungated updates, and expanded replay sets on fresh traffic (§§3.3, 4.1).
3. **Can structured execution preserve long-workflow fidelity?** Personalization examples show prose skills losing detail over long constructions. Next experiment: compare prose skills with structured plans and deterministic subroutines on matched precision tasks (Appendix I).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and reported comparisons, but lacks human validation, most uncertainty estimates, full replay hyperparameters, exact Graphic-Eval model versions, and confirmation of code or skill-bank release. It also leaves unexplained why the gallery covers 193 prompts while the main internal evaluation uses 200.
