# Retention-Constrained Post-Training Quantization of Cellpose-SAM for Stem Cell Microscopy

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.21038) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21038)


## One-sentence takeaway
On a 176-field microscopy panel, sensitivity-guided mixed W4/W8 quantization reduced Cellpose–SAM weight storage by 6.76× while passing per-modality F1 retention criteria, but poor FP32 performance on iPSC images prevents interpreting this as demonstrated iPSC segmentation utility (§4).

## Problem and motivation
Laboratory CPU and edge deployments need smaller segmentation models, but aggregate accuracy can hide modality-specific degradation. The author addresses this evaluation gap with a pre-specified, downstream retention test comparing compressed Cellpose–SAM against FP32, accompanied by failure reporting and audit artifacts (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Retention protocol:** Require the entire 95% cluster-bootstrap interval for mean paired instance-F1 change at IoU 0.5 to exceed −0.02 separately for every modality. Resampling uses experimental units, with 2,000 draws; tensor-level diagnostics do not determine acceptance (§3).
- **Sensitivity-guided compression:** Perturb 100 eligible Linear/Conv2d operators individually at W4 on a disjoint development split, then retain the four most sensitive operators at INT8 while quantizing the remainder to W4 (§3; Table 3 note).
- **Deployment-focused comparison:** Evaluate weight-only, calibrated activation-instrumented, mixed-precision, and ternary variants using downstream retention, catastrophic failures, and weight storage. The contribution is principally the evaluation protocol and its application to this model; the text claims released audit artifacts but supplies no accessible artifact location (§§1–4).

The results support relative retention for the tested schemes and panel. They do not establish useful absolute iPSC segmentation: the author explicitly says FP32 is unreliable on high-density iPSC and that NIST results are floor-limited (§4). Storage ratios exclude retained FP32 scale and reconstruction metadata, and no CPU latency, runtime memory, or energy measurements are supplied (Table 3 caption).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Verdicts do not transfer automatically to other imaging modalities, decoders, or tighter release-testing margins (§6). The margin was internally pre-specified, not externally registered (§2).
- **Author-stated baseline limitation:** A workflow-specific iPSC dataset with a competent FP32 reference is needed before deployment claims are justified (§4).
- **Analyst inference—limited independent iPSC evidence:** The NIST arm contains 72 tiles but only three source-image experimental units, limiting the breadth of independent evidence (§3).
- **Analyst inference—unclear failure statistics:** W4 and W8A16-QDQ-obs each reportedly fail on 1/176 fields, yet their reported intervals are [0.000, 0.001]. The relationship between these field counts and experimental-unit rate estimates is insufficiently explained to reconcile them (§4; Table 2 caption).
- **Analyst inference—unresolved mixed-precision benefit:** Zero failures for mixed precision versus one for plain W4 does not establish superiority at this sample size. No alternative exception-count or operator-selection ablation is supplied.
- **Reproducibility gaps:** The retrieved text includes table captions and narrative results but omits table bodies, the numerical catastrophic-quality floor, detailed calibration settings, and an artifact link.

## Open research questions
1. **Does retention hold when FP32 reliably segments iPSCs?** Current NIST results are floor-limited. Test a workflow-specific, independently annotated panel spanning density regimes with adequate FP32 performance.
2. **Do the four INT8 exceptions materially improve reliability over plain W4?** Current failure counts provide limited separation. Compare different exception counts and selection strategies on a larger independent panel.
3. **Do storage savings translate into laboratory hardware gains?** The paper reports weight-blob compression rather than measured deployment performance. Benchmark latency, peak memory, and complete artifact size on target CPUs and edge devices.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the main relative-retention findings, but detailed verification is constrained by missing table bodies, absolute baseline scores, numerical AP endpoints, the catastrophic-failure threshold, development/calibration details, executable audit artifacts, and hardware performance measurements.
