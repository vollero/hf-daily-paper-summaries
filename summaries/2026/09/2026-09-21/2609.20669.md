# Learning Foresight without Explicit Trajectories for 3D Diffusion Policies

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20669) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20669)


## One-sentence takeaway
Movement Trend Guidance improves reported manipulation success by training a compact latent on future gripper states and using it to condition a 3D diffusion policy without executing predicted waypoints.

## Problem and motivation
Point-cloud diffusion policies must learn both immediate geometric control and longer-term interaction structure through action prediction. The authors argue that explicit trajectories impose unnecessarily precise intermediate targets that can constrain correction when predictions are wrong. Their proposed gap is future-aware conditioning that preserves observation-driven, receding-horizon control (§I).

## Approach and contributions
- **Future-supervised representation:** A three-frame point-cloud and robot-state history produces a 256-dimensional latent. An auxiliary decoder predicts gripper position and gripper state at four future offsets; its outputs are discarded at inference (§III-A–D).
- **Controlled conditioning:** The latent enters global observation conditioning, with an additional gated FiLM branch only at the diffusion UNet bottleneck. The extra branch starts as an identity mapping; dense action prediction and receding-horizon execution are preserved (§III-B–D).
- **Empirical attribution:** Matched comparisons and ablations examine future supervision, explicit-point conditioning, injection location, and target offsets. A separate ACT experiment tests applicability beyond diffusion policies (§IV–V).

The claimed distinction from related foresight methods is the combination of task-space future supervision, latent-only policy conditioning, and restricted additional modulation (§II-C).

These results support the method’s usefulness in the reported settings, particularly the contribution of future supervision beyond added capacity. They do not directly establish that the latent represents interaction intent or that bottleneck modulation preserves local correction; those are the authors’ interpretations of performance differences.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Arm occlusion can remove critical object geometry and impair both local control and trend estimation. Broader physical validation across objects, scenes, and perturbations remains open (§IV-E).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** LIBERO deviations measure rollout-seed variability, not independently trained-model variability. RoboTwin ablations report one shared-policy run per variant, limiting conclusions about training stability (§IV-C; §V).
- **Analyst inference—checkpoint selection:** DexArt’s best-five-checkpoint aggregation introduces selection effects; its reporting differs from LIBERO’s fixed final checkpoint (§IV-A).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** ACT evidence covers only the separate six-task setting. Physical evaluation covers one platform and five tasks (§IV-D; §V-E).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism and tuning:** Observation-history length remains fixed, and temporal offsets have different meanings across benchmarks. The text does not isolate history-length effects or test consistent physical-time horizons (§III-D; §V-C).
- **Comparison clarity:** Retained official RoboTwin baseline rows and newly matched mixed-training rows represent different settings; the starred DP3 comparison provides the clearest matched evidence (Table I; Appendix II).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the gain persist across training seeds?** Reported rollout variation does not answer this. Repeat matched DP3 and future-loss ablations with independently trained policies.
2. **Can trend conditioning tolerate missing geometry?** Occlusion is an identified failure source. Evaluate controlled point-cloud dropout and arm occlusion, comparing success and future-target prediction error.
3. **How should supervision horizons transfer across sampling rates?** Offsets index different temporal representations across benchmarks. Compare fixed-time, fixed-frame, and adaptive offsets under controlled subsampling.
4. **Does the method generalize to unseen physical conditions?** The real-robot study leaves broader validation open. Test held-out objects, scene layouts, and external perturbations with matched demonstration budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and reports extensive comparisons, but broader generalization and training reliability remain uncertain.
