# Calibrating Teacher--Student Discrepancy for On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.21619) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21619)


## One-sentence takeaway
Cal-OPD filters teacher–student likelihood discrepancies using contextual probes, improving reported average mathematical-reasoning accuracy over standard OPD in both tested Qwen3 configurations, with training speed gains limited to the smaller teacher setting (Table 3; Appendix A.8).

## Problem and motivation
On-policy distillation (OPD) trains on student-generated responses using token-level teacher–student log-likelihood differences. The authors argue that these differences mix transferable capability with **teacher self-deviation (TSD)**: changes in teacher likelihood caused by context while the evaluated student trajectory stays fixed.

Their identified gap is that existing supervision-reliability methods do not explicitly estimate a contextual-variation interval around the teacher reference. Directly distilling a teacher given privileged information may amplify this variation (§§1–2; Appendix A.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Empirical characterization:** Controlled teacher-context interventions reveal likelihood shifts without task-specific information, substantial agreement between contrasting interventions, and concentration on discourse expressions rather than numerical or symbolic forms (§2).
- **Calibration method:** Score each student token under the original teacher context and positive/negative interventions. Expand the observed upward/downward deviations by a relaxation factor, then retain only the signed discrepancy beyond the nearest interval boundary; assign zero advantage inside it (§3, Eqs. 13–14).
- **Evaluation and controls:** Compare against five OPD baselines, alternative interventions, calibration strengths, and signal-reduction controls. The default uses contrasting evaluative statements and relaxation factor λ = 5; these statements probe the teacher rather than directly supervise the student (§4; Appendices A.6, A.9).

These results support improved benchmark accuracy under the tested recipes and show that uniform attenuation or random masking does not reproduce the gain. They do **not** establish that removed discrepancies contain only non-useful knowledge: the authors themselves interpret solution-based over-filtering as removing useful supervision (§4.3). The small advantage over the strongest hard-filter control lacks reported uncertainty.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged:** Two interventions only approximate the teacher’s contextual variability; broadening the interval too far removes useful supervision (§§2.1, 3–4.3).
- **Author-acknowledged:** Two extra teacher forward passes make Cal-OPD slower with the larger teacher (Appendix A.8).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization remains uncertain beyond Qwen3 thinking models, mathematical tasks, and the solution-filtered training subset (§4.1; Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Context sensitivity and token-form rankings do not independently establish whether each removed signal is irrelevant to reasoning. No direct token-level knowledge-quality annotation is reported (§2; Appendix A.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Comparisons include implementation differences: ExOPD uses LoRA, while Uni-OPD changes question/rollout allocation and filtering. Equal training steps therefore do not isolate the objective alone (Appendix A.6).
- **Reporting gaps:** Training-seed variation, confidence intervals, and calibration-selection protocol are not reported. How top-16 teacher log-probabilities handle sampled tokens outside that set is unclear (Appendix A.6).

## Open research questions
- **Does calibration transfer across model families and tasks?** Evaluation stays within Qwen3 mathematics; test independent teacher/student families on additional reasoning domains.
- **Can interval width adapt without discarding useful guidance?** Fixed intervention choices and large λ values can hurt; compare adaptive widths against fixed λ using a separate validation set.
- **Does the residual mechanism reliably beat hard filtering?** The strongest control is close and lacks uncertainty estimates; repeat both methods across training seeds with paired benchmark uncertainty.
- **Does Cal-OPD improve accuracy per compute budget with large teachers?** Larger configurations incur substantial overhead; compare learning curves at equal wall time or GPU-hours.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and reported comparisons clearly; causal interpretation and broader validity remain less secure.

Material missing information includes code/checkpoint availability, training-seed variability, statistical uncertainty, hyperparameter-selection procedures, exact retention matching for the hard-filter control, and implementation details connecting truncated teacher log-probabilities to the stated objective.
