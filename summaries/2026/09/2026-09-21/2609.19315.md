# GAVEL: Graph World Models for Verified and Efficient Long-Horizon LLM Task Planning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19315) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19315)


## One-sentence takeaway
GAVEL combines symbolic plan repair with belief-aware task ordering, raising reported Qwen3-8B success substantially on BEHAVIOR-1K-derived symbolic benchmarks while leaving physical manipulation feasibility unverified (§§V–VI).

## Problem and motivation
LLM-generated robot plans can omit prerequisites, violate action constraints, or fail to achieve goals. Sending every error back to the LLM adds cost even when an explicit action model already determines the correction. Conversely, graph-edit planners struggle with semantic procedures such as operating an appliance to wash an object. Under partial observability, fixed task orders also miss opportunities to exploit newly discovered object locations (§I).

## Approach and contributions
- **Verification with direct repair:** An explicit graph represents object relations, semantic states, and action preconditions/effects. GAVEL simulates proposed plans, inserts model-derived corrections, checks goals and restoration constraints, and returns unresolved failures to the LLM (§§IV-E–F).
- **Distributional task ordering:** Room-location beliefs produce expected search costs. GAVEL validates candidate task compositions, executes the first task, updates beliefs, and reorders remaining tasks (§§III-B, IV-G).
- **Integrated evaluation:** Repair-only, feedback-only, belief-collapse, and fixed-order variants isolate contributions within a shared pipeline. Task extraction uses shared LoRA adapters; belief initialization adopts SEEK’s RSN formulation rather than introducing that component (§§IV-B–C, V-C).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Repair and feedback improve single-task success | On 100 tasks, Qwen3-8B success: LLM-only **41.2%**, feedback-only **76.4%**, repair-only **76.2%**, full GAVEL **91.8%**. Feedback-only averages **2.7** LLM calls versus **1.5** for GAVEL. | §V-E; Table I referenced, body unavailable |
| Multi-task reliability improves | On 500 partially observed instructions using Qwen3-8B: LLM-only **19.9%**, SayPlan-style **75.6%**, EPoG-style **60.2%**, GAVEL **92.6%** success. | §V-F; Table II referenced, body unavailable |
| Travel improves against feedback-only planning | On matched instructions both methods completed, SayPlan-style travel is **83.01 m**, versus **78.01 m** for GAVEL. | §V-F |
| Beliefs and reordering each reduce travel | Mean distance: point-estimate/fixed-order **82.45 m**, distributional/fixed-order **79.69 m**, distributional/online **78.01 m**. Authors report approximately **5.4%** combined savings against the point-estimate variant. | §V-F |
| Explicit modeling remains useful with stronger planners | On the same 100-instruction multi-task subset, Qwen3-4B + GAVEL achieves **75.2%**, versus **24.6%** for GPT-5.6 Sol alone and **38.6%** for Claude Sonnet 5 alone. Hosted models + GAVEL are described as “near-perfect,” without exact rates in the supplied prose. | §V-G |
| Executor agreement supports the abstraction locally | Success outcomes agree between the lightweight executor and OmniGibson for all **100 single-task instructions** tested. | §V-A |

The authors state that results average five random seeds (§V-A). These comparisons support the utility of repair and uncertainty-aware ordering within the evaluated symbolic pipeline. They do not establish physical robot reliability or globally optimal execution policies: task ordering minimizes an approximate cost model (§III-B).

The abstract’s attribution of approximately 5.4% savings to comparison with a “static variant” is ambiguous: §V-F’s detailed values attach that combined improvement to **gavel-map**, not distributional **gavel-static**.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Symbolic manipulation omits motion-level reachability and collision constraints. Priors primarily reflect room types, and instruction grounding remains a major failure source (§VI).
- **Analyst inference—restricted scope:** Known layouts, independent goal-object sets, and small task sets limit evidence for unknown maps, dependent tasks, or larger schedules (§§III-A–B).
- **Analyst inference—conditional verification:** Checks depend on supplied action semantics and extracted goals; success against those representations does not itself establish fidelity to physical dynamics or user intent (§§IV-B, IV-E–F).
- **Comparison boundary:** SayPlan and EPoG mechanisms are reproduced inside the shared pipeline; results do not constitute direct evaluations of their original complete implementations (§V-C).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The listed residual-failure categories account for 35 of 38 failures, with the remainder and seed aggregation unspecified (§V-F).

## Open research questions
- **Does repair transfer to physical manipulation?** Geometric feasibility is excluded (§VI). Add reachability and collision checks, then compare symbolic validity with execution success in cluttered manipulation trials.
- **How does ordering scale to dependent, larger task sets?** Experiments use small independent sets (§§III-A–B). Evaluate dependency-constrained scheduling with shared objects and compare approximate ordering against exhaustive search where tractable.
- **How much does grounding cap success?** Extraction dominates the reported residual failures (§V-F). Compare learned extraction with oracle objects and goals while holding planning fixed.
- **How robust are beliefs to missed detections?** Completed searches eliminate rooms from belief support (§IV-G). Inject controlled detection failures and compare hard elimination with probabilistic observation updates.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and headline comparisons, but Tables I–III contain captions without their numerical bodies.

Material gaps include reported standard deviations, exact hosted-model GAVEL success rates, full failure accounting, matched-success denominators for ordering ablations, detailed prompts and training settings, hardware specifications, and code/data/checkpoint release availability.
