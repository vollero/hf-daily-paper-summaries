# RecreationWorld: Scalable and Verifiable Environments for Hybrid Computer-Use Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.22000) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22000)


## One-sentence takeaway
RecreationWorld turns executable applications into five-platform training and evaluation tasks, reporting transfer gains from verified recreation trajectories while finding that even the strongest evaluated agent rarely passes an application’s entire programmatic suite (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
GUI operation and software development are usually evaluated separately, leaving agents’ ability to coordinate exploration, coding, execution, and visual verification undermeasured. The authors propose application recreation as a setting where agents must discover requirements through interaction and deliver working software, while the running reference supplies observable outcomes for evaluation (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Cross-platform recreation framework:** Persistent, isolated workers expose native GUI controls and development tools on Ubuntu, macOS, Windows, Android, and Web, allowing agents to choose their own explore–implement–verify workflow (§2).
- **Reference-grounded evaluation:** Generated tests couple actions with programmatic or visual outcomes, undergo reference replay and human review, and become fixed hidden suites. RecreationBench contains 250 tasks, equally divided across platforms (§4).
- **Verified trajectory training:** Qwen3.8-Max generates recreation rollouts; verifier-based rejection sampling selects 35,000 trajectories for supervised fine-tuning of two model initializations (§3.1).
- **Empirical characterization:** The paper measures transfer, behavioral fidelity, workflow patterns, artifact structure, integrity failures, and an auxiliary programmable-runtime comparison (§§3, 5–6). The contribution is their integration around recreation; the supplied text does not establish that every component is individually novel.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

Astra passes the complete programmatic suite on 2.8% of applications. | Table 3; §5.1 |
| Both training arms improve across all five transfer benchmarks | First-to-last evaluated checkpoints improve on every benchmark. These results support recreation as a measurable training and evaluation setting and provide exploratory evidence of transfer. They do not establish full application equivalence, causal benefits from particular verification behaviors, or an isolated runtime effect. Scores characterize complete model–harness configurations, and the transfer benchmarks use different metrics and budgets.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Pinned environments exclude much live-service, changing-state, and multi-user work; finite tests cannot establish equivalence across all behaviors (§8).
- **Author-stated comparison limits:** Transfer evaluations use single trials and include budget/harness differences. Workflow correlations and the runtime comparison are not controlled causal experiments (§§3.3, 5.2; Appendix A.5).
- **Author-stated integrity gaps:** Exact-line overlap cannot rule out transformed memorization. Android lacks attested packet-level egress filtering, and Web case studies show transformed reference replay escaping detection (§6.4; Appendices C.6, D.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Recreation-specific training benefits remain difficult to isolate without matched alternative-data and unfiltered-trajectory controls, which are not reported (§3).
- **Analyst inference:** Judge reliability and implementation independence need further validation: human review verifies tests on references, but candidate-level VLM agreement rates are not reported, and structured checks depend on accessibility conventions (§4.4; Appendix C.4).
- **Reproducibility qualification:** Android source revision identifiers are mostly inferred from archive timestamps rather than verified build revisions (Appendix C.1).

## Open research questions
1. **Which training ingredients drive transfer?** The shared SFT mixture does not isolate recreation or verifier selection. Compare equally budgeted recreation, ordinary coding, and unfiltered recreation datasets.
2. **Does enforced final verification improve fidelity?** Final-loop closure is descriptive and does not track model ranking consistently. Randomize a final relaunch-and-check requirement under a fixed execution budget.
3. **How well do frozen suites generalize to unseen behaviors?** Finite coverage and replay-detector failures leave uncertainty. Evaluate submissions on independently authored interaction sequences and transformed-copy adversarial cases.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives substantial methodological detail, but stronger causal and reproducibility conclusions require SFT hyperparameters, trajectory-selection thresholds and acceptance rates, repeated-run uncertainty, candidate-level judge validation, and clearer accounting of missing evaluations. Actual release availability and release of training trajectories or trained checkpoints are not established by the supplied text.
