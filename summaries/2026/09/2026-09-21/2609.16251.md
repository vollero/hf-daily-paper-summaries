# CADWorld: Computer-Use Benchmark for Long-Horizon Computer-Aided Design

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.16251) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16251)


## One-sentence takeaway
CADWorld evaluates GUI agents through executable checks on saved FreeCAD projects, reporting 17.5% best-agent success versus an 87.0% expert reference on 200 tasks, although the introduction describes a different evaluation subset (Abstract; §§1, 4).

## Problem and motivation
Mechanical CAD requires precise geometry, constraints, feature histories, and valid downstream engineering state across long interaction sequences. Visually plausible output can still be unusable. The authors identify a gap between broad desktop benchmarks and programmatic CAD evaluations: neither systematically tests GUI execution that leaves persistent, editable engineering artifacts (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **CAD workflow benchmark:** 200 authored tasks covering 11 workflow categories and 183 concepts, including modeling, assembly, manufacturing, simulation, inspection, and documentation (§3).
- **Reproducible interaction environment:** FreeCAD in an Ubuntu VM, with screenshot observations, restricted GUI actions, stateful keyboard–mouse sequences, and recorded trajectories. Standard runs allow 100 model steps; each step can contain multiple actions (§3; Appendix D).
- **Artifact-grounded evaluation:** Task-specific checks inspect saved `.FCStd` projects and auxiliary outputs for geometry, constraints, document structure, CAM state, and FEM results. Success requires every check to pass; diagnostics retain finer-grained errors (§3).
- **Baseline and failure analysis:** Seven agents are evaluated to characterize completion, efficiency, and failure stages. The contribution is an evaluation framework, not a new agent-training method (§§1, 4).

These results support low reliability under the reported CADWorld conditions and show why appearance alone is insufficient. They do not establish causal explanations for failures, population-level human performance, or a general advantage of GUI execution over scripting. Missing result-table cells prevent independent reconstruction of rankings and ablation comparisons.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Explicit goals and supplied assets omit requirement elicitation, incomplete source designs, and open-ended engineering trade-offs; FreeCAD alone limits software coverage (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Interfaces, context limits, infrastructure, and latency differ across agents. Human results represent one expert reference pass, not a controlled human study (§§4.1, 6).
- **Analyst inference:** Input comparability is imperfect: some adapters omit reference images under image-input constraints. This could affect comparisons on image-dependent tasks (Appendix K).
- **Analyst inference:** The unexplained full-suite/subset reporting difference and underspecified harness ablation weaken quantitative interpretation (§§1, 4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Assigning the first applicable failure category identifies an observed failure stage, not necessarily its root cause; early failures can conceal downstream errors (Appendix E).
- **Evidence boundary:** The proposed training-exposure explanation is explicitly speculative. Likewise, the terminal ablation does not establish that scripted construction necessarily prevents native editability (§4.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Does more interaction context or budget improve completion?** The reported setup fixes a short recent history and bounded execution without a sensitivity study. Next experiment: vary those limits on matched tasks and track success plus failure-stage changes (Table 6).
2. **Can explicit artifact verification improve engineering correctness?** Stronger agents reach saved artifacts but fail semantic checks. Next experiment: compare baseline agents with agents given structured verification-and-repair feedback under matched budgets (§4.2).
3. **What causes terminal-generated structure failures?** The ablation does not isolate scripting from construction strategy. Next experiment: compare GUI and terminal agents on identical tasks with explicit native-history requirements and matched evaluator checks (§4.2).
4. **Do these failure patterns transfer across CAD applications?** Only FreeCAD is evaluated. Next experiment: recreate matched workflows and artifact checks in another CAD suite (§6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The benchmark design and broad failure pattern are clearly described, but quantitative interpretation is constrained.

Material missing information includes most result-table cells, exact category counts, complete model configurations, evaluator tolerances and validation evidence, repeated-run uncertainty, matched ablation details, the explanation for the two evaluation scopes, and how the reported expert success reference relates to the partially documented demonstration set.
