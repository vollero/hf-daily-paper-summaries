# MLLMs Hallucinate when Information Distribution Drifts in Synergy Heads

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.09206) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09206)


## One-sentence takeaway
HEAL reduces reported visual hallucinations by rebalancing visual and language contributions within selected attention heads during inference, with substantial decoding overhead and evidence for an intervenable mechanism rather than a universal explanation (§§3–4; Table 12).

## Problem and motivation
MLLMs can generate fluent responses inconsistent with an image. The authors argue that attention weights alone inadequately describe modality contributions, while indiscriminately strengthening vision can impair language generation. They investigate whether interactions inside individual attention heads offer a more precise intervention target (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Head taxonomy:** Replace head outputs with noise to identify heads with negligible effects on layer representations. Then evaluate four factual/counterfactual modality combinations, using cosine-based differences to classify heads as redundant, visual, language, or synergy (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Mechanistic finding:** The authors associate hallucinated tokens with greater language dominance inside synergy heads and report changes in head roles during generation. Manipulating modality balance provides intervention evidence supporting this association (§§1, 4.5; Figures 1–2, 6).
- **Inference-time calibration:** Scale visual and language value vectors in synergy heads toward a chosen equilibrium factor. Head types update periodically; calibration factors update each decoding step. Batched counterfactual computation reduces overhead (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Qualified theoretical account:** Value scaling has the claimed information-calibration interpretation under local additivity, positive homogeneity, and linear approximation. The scores are operational representation measures, not exact Shannon information or a complete PID decomposition (Appendices A–B).

These results support useful mitigation across the tested models and benchmarks. They do not establish that synergy-head drift explains every hallucination or that HEAL dominates every metric: EAH has lower CHAIR hallucination scores. The causal evidence combines output examples with aggregate benchmark changes; it does not quantify how universally the proposed mechanism accounts for individual errors.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Equilibrium factors remain empirical; excessive visual calibration can degrade grammar and generation quality. Missing evidence or early visual encoding failures cannot be repaired by this intervention (§4.4; §5).
- **Author-stated:** Fixed update intervals delay adaptation, and existing evaluations do not isolate abrupt switches between visual grounding and linguistic reasoning (Appendix D.3).
- **Theoretical scope:** HEAL’s interaction score conflates synergy and redundancy under its proposed information analogy. Theorem 1 depends on local approximations; Theorem 2’s proof asserts a positive directional effect in expectation. **Analyst inference:** this does not establish unconditional monotonic alignment for arbitrary representations after normalization (Appendices A–B).
- **Analyst inference:** Aggregate gains do not isolate the benefit of targeting synergy heads against matched random-head or all-head calibration; such controls are not reported.
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Table 1 and Table 2 give slightly different HEAL results for LLaVA-1.5-7B without explaining the discrepancy. The supplied text does not define the reported ± quantities or repetition counts.
- **Deployment scope:** Efficiency measurements use one model and a small caption sample; clinical use is motivation, not a demonstrated application (§1; Appendix C.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Does synergy-head selection specifically drive the gains?** The reported controls do not resolve this. Compare synergy, random, modality-specific, and all-head interventions with matched calibration strength and computational budgets.
2. **Can calibration adapt without benchmark-specific tuning?** A shared empirical equilibrium factor remains necessary. Compare fixed settings with token- or head-adaptive policies on held-out tasks, measuring hallucination and language quality.
3. **When do periodic updates miss critical transitions?** The authors acknowledge that current benchmarks do not isolate this case. Construct prompts alternating visual description and linguistic reasoning; compare periodic and event-triggered updates using localized error rates and latency.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method description and benchmark gains; its stronger causal and theoretical interpretations remain conditional.

Material missing information includes code/release availability, detailed evaluation prompts and splits, seed and repetition counts, uncertainty definitions, token-level hallucination annotation procedures, matched head-selection controls, and actual figure graphics beyond their captions.
