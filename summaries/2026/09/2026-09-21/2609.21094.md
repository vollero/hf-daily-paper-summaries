# Geometry of Values: Task Vector Composition for Ethical Preference Alignment in Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.21094) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21094)


## One-sentence takeaway
Separating an estimated instruction-following direction from fine-tuning weight changes enables ethical preference reversal in compact Llama models, but failed transitive composition limits the evidence to local, pairwise modularity (§§4.4, 6).

## Problem and motivation
The authors study whether small language models can learn priorities between conflicting ethical values and subsequently reverse those priorities through weight manipulation. They identify a gap in multilingual ethics evaluation: direct conflicts between principles receive less attention than stereotypes and toxicity, while prompt-based policy adherence can be uneven across languages (§§1–2).

The task measures adherence to a specified stance—not whether a decision is morally correct independently of that stance.

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled dilemma benchmark:** Create 12,000 English dilemmas across Honesty–Justice, Justice–Autonomy, and Autonomy–Honesty, with parallel translations into Hindi, Arabic, Spanish, and Chinese. Each pair has 3,200 training, 400 development, and 400 test examples. GPT-4o generates examples; Gemini validation proposes corrections to 4,055 examples (§3).
- **Compact-model alignment evaluation:** Train Llama-3.2 1B/3B using LoRA SFT or DPO on stance-consistent choices, then evaluate synthetic and human-written dilemmas. GPT–5-MINI supplies unprompted-preference and explicit-policy baselines (§§4–5).
- **Preference reversal through task vectors:** Average weight deltas from two opposing auxiliary stances to estimate an instruction direction, remove the target stance delta’s projection onto that direction, and subtract the remaining preference component while adding instruction capacity. Mixing coefficients are selected on development data; reversal requires previously trained checkpoints (§4.4, Algorithm 1).
- **Boundary on composition:** Reversal works substantially better than chaining independently learned preferences transitively, providing evidence for limited modularity (§6).

The evidence supports learning these operationalized preferences and reversing them within the studied models. It does not establish general moral reasoning, freedom from textual shortcuts, or a fully compositional representation of values. The human set provides preliminary transfer evidence; near-orthogonal vectors alone do not establish why transitive composition fails.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Only three principles, forced-choice responses, and two compact Llama sizes are studied. Translations test linguistic robustness, not culturally equivalent moral interpretation (§§3.3, 6.1).
- **Author-stated data risks:** Generated and machine-translated examples may retain artifacts; the human gold set is small (§6.1).
- **Author-stated resource dependency:** Reversal avoids new target-policy training but still requires aligned checkpoints and development-set tuning (§6.1).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism remains provisional:** Averaging opposing stance deltas is assumed to isolate instruction following. The supplied evidence does not independently verify that interpretation or rule out residual preference information (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference—comparisons do not prove necessity:** Prompted GPT–5-MINI and fine-tuned Llama differ in model and training setup. Their comparison cannot establish that fine-tuning is universally required for robust policy shifts (§§4–5).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Broad claims of at least 90% gold accuracy overlook DPO’s 85% cell; claims of at least 97% 3B retention outside Hindi AB overlook 96% cells. Figures provide the more specific evidence (Figs. 3–4; §§6–7).

## Open research questions
1. **Does reversal survive broader human evaluation?** The gold set is small and translations are not culturally localized. Test independently authored, locally reviewed dilemmas with additional principles and report uncertainty.
2. **What does the estimated instruction vector actually encode?** Its interpretation rests on averaging opposing checkpoints. Compare against a checkpoint trained explicitly for response formatting, including projection-free ablations and invalid-output rates.
3. **Why does transitive composition fail?** Near-random outcomes and vector angles provide limited diagnostic evidence. Compare linear composition with jointly trained transitive policies and nonlinear combinations on held-out value triples.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and central results are clear, but mechanistic and generalization claims exceed the available evidence.

Material gaps include numerical DPO seed variability, detailed transitivity results, quantitative naive-reversal failures, annotation agreement and participant background, translation-audit coverage, compute costs, and confirmed release contents. Some plotted numerical details are unavailable in the retrieved text.
