# SteerDuplex: Steerable Duplex Speech Dialogue Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.12623) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12623)


## One-sentence takeaway
Targeted supervised training substantially improves requested vocal delivery on SteerBench, while subsequent reinforcement learning improves selected interruption and pause metrics but leaves premature yielding and evaluation-selection concerns unresolved.

## Problem and motivation
Full-duplex speech assistants must control both **what they say and how they sound**, while deciding when to speak, wait, or yield. The authors identify a gap between fluent conversational timing and reliable instruction-based control of tone, persona, style, and speaking pace. Transcript-only evaluation cannot establish acoustic compliance (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Steering taxonomy and benchmark:** The taxonomy separates instruction-based steering, acoustic adaptation, and duplex interaction. SteerBench tests a narrower subset through 390 single-turn English prompts and 1,067 binary rubrics, separating content from delivery judged against fixed reference audio (§4; Appendix H).
- **Targeted supervised training:** SteerDuplex retains Moshi’s architecture and adds system-prompt conditioning, training on natural conversations and targeted speech/text examples covering steering, instruction following, reasoning, safety, and interaction (§3.1).
- **Continuity-aware RL:** Two-stage GDPO combines interaction timing, transcript-judge rewards, response continuity, and waveform checks; the second stage adds continuation rewards and user-backchannel sampling. Policy loss applies to text actions, including padding, without direct audio-codebook policy loss. Reference-audio steering rubrics are evaluation-only (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Reward-failure analysis:** Separate probes demonstrate empty-output shortcuts, while training diagnostics show that stronger interruption rewards can coexist with weaker response continuation (§7).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| SFT substantially improves acoustic steering | SteerBench audio APR: **65.10 ± 1.13%**, versus Moshi **20.55%** and PersonaPlex **16.44%**, with matched items, rubrics, and Gemini judge; SFT averages three selected runs. | Table 10; §8 |
| Complete compliance remains difficult | SFT sample APR, requiring all text/audio criteria: tone **49.67%**, persona **51.11%**, style/accent **48.40%**, speed/length **32%**. | Table 10 |
| Multi-turn task success improves but remains low | AudioMC SFT APR **13.64 ± 0.28%**, versus PersonaPlex **6.64%**; ARS **37.17 ± 1.33%** versus **20.56%**. Baselines retain source protocols. | Table 1a |
| RL improves selected overlap responses | Source-clean FDB-v1.5: interruption success **72.5% → 82.5%** on 200 items; user-backchannel continuation **71.4% → 80.6%** on 98 items; one decoding pass per checkpoint. | Table 11; §8 |
| RL reduces synthetic pause barge-in | SFT **26.5% → 9%** after RL, averaged across three runs on source-clean synthetic pause items. | §6.2; Figure 4 |
| Aggregate task retention masks category losses | FDB-v2 slow-examiner mean remains **4.17/5**, but daily-task score falls **3.74 → 3.41**; 200 sessions per pass, three runs per model. | Table 12 |
| Isolated rewards permit empty speech | Separate probes: **25–47 empty outputs/96** for isolated objectives, versus **4/96** for the joint composite; shared duplex diagnostic **0–0.055** versus **0.371**. | Table 9; Appendix E |
| Reference audio helps, but judging remains imperfect | Agreement with 200 corresponding human annotations is approximately **76%** with references versus **62%** without. | Appendix D.5 |

These results support improved steering under the paper’s controlled evaluation and selective interaction gains after RL. They do not establish uniformly preserved conversational behavior, unrestricted personalization, or a causal advantage from staging alone. Reported dispersion is population standard deviation across selected decoding runs, not a confidence interval.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Selection bias, acknowledged:** Repeated evaluations use the three highest-scoring runs from pools of unequal size. This can inflate reported means; narrow dispersion does not address that bias (§8).
- **Scope and overlap, acknowledged:** SteerBench covers single-turn English requests with fixed references. Some official FDB-v1 turn/pause material overlaps supervised CANDOR data and is treated as diagnostic (§§5, 8; Appendix H).
- **Incomplete causal isolation, acknowledged:** Controls differ in training budget, data, and configuration. Backbone, data, reward components, and staging effects are not independently isolated (§7.1; Appendix C).
- **Behavioral and judge sensitivity, acknowledged:** RL sometimes yields prematurely. Both judges find a turn-taking loss in a common rescored pool, unlike the small positive difference in the broader comparison. SteerBench and AudioMC lack corresponding second-judge studies (§§7.4, 8).
- **Analyst inference—aggregation ambiguity:** Table 5’s SFT SteerBench rubric pass rate is below every category rubric rate in Table 10. The text does not explain the differing aggregation or evaluation population, limiting interpretation of the RL steering comparison.
- **Reproducibility constraints:** Release is promised upon acceptance; proprietary interaction data and restricted recordings are not redistributed. Latency distributions beyond means are unavailable (§8; Appendices G–H).

## Open research questions
1. **Does staging itself improve interaction?** Existing controls have unequal budgets. Compare single-stage and two-stage RL with matched data, sampling, updates, and compute.
2. **Can yielding improve without sacrificing completeness?** Premature yielding persists despite continuity rewards. Evaluate matched policies on paired interruptions and listener backchannels, scoring both floor management and answer completion.
3. **Does steering generalize beyond fixed references and single turns?** SteerBench does not test long-term personalization. Test sustained and revised instructions across conversations using multiple valid reference styles and independent human ratings.
4. **How much do run selection and judge choice affect gains?** Selected-run reporting and limited judge checks leave uncertainty. Preregister decoding seeds, report all runs, and rescore steering with multiple judges and blinded human evaluations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main reported comparisons, but selection bias and protocol ambiguity weaken their interpretation.

Material missing information includes complete run pools and selection details, budget-matched ablations, an explanation of the SteerBench aggregation discrepancy, latency distributions, broader human validation, and currently available release artifacts.
