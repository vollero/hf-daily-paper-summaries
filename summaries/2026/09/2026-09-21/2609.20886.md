# BI-Agent and BI-Bench: Towards Automating End-to-End Business Intelligence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20886) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20886)


## One-sentence takeaway
BI-Agent combines specialized data-management tools with synthetic-trajectory post-training to improve raw-table-to-answer accuracy on BI-Bench, although substantial errors remain and evaluation covers a restricted BI setting (Tables 3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Answering business questions from raw tables requires selecting relevant data, reshaping tables, discovering joins, and computing results. The authors argue that existing NL2SQL benchmarks insufficiently test these preparation steps, while conventional BI workflows require considerable manual expertise (§§1–3).

The target is ad-hoc analysis over unprepared tables, producing a correct result table—not dashboard generation or the full enterprise BI lifecycle (§3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **BI-Bench:** A manually verified benchmark of 100 questions from 82 public Power BI projects, with answers exported from dashboards and alternative valid output schemas reviewed by experts. The authors claim it is the first benchmark specifically targeting end-to-end BI (§4; §6.1, footnote 9).
- **BI-Agent:** An LLM orchestrates code execution, LLM-based table selection, and existing algorithms for table reshaping and join prediction. The contribution is their system-level integration, rather than new underlying transformation or join algorithms (§5.1).
- **BI-specific post-training:** The pipeline samples project sub-schemas, materializes joined tables, synthesizes questions and answers, and introduces irrelevant tables and inverse reshaping operations. Verified teacher trajectories support SFT; GRPO then rewards correct result tables, with intermediate credit for returning a table and penalties for syntax errors. Policy updates target tool-call blocks (§5.2).

## Evidence and main results
BI-Bench results average ten runs. Accuracy compares output shape and cell values, permits row/column permutations, normalizes formatting and missing values, and uses numeric tolerances (§6.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

| Strongest reported BI-Bench accuracy remains limited | o4-mini improves from 48.2% to 61.9% with SQL tools, and from 57.4% to 66.3% with Python tools. SQL gain: 13.7 points, 95% CI [7.7, 19.7]. | Table 3 |
| Largest tool gain starts from a low baseline | GPT-OSS-120B SQL accuracy rises from 5.3% to 45.3%, a 40.0-point gain, 95% CI [33.3, 46.8]. | Table 3 |

The evidence supports substantial improvements within the tested settings. Significance findings survive project-level analysis and false-discovery correction (§6.1, footnote 9). It does not establish reliable autonomous enterprise deployment or accuracy parity with the strongest tool-equipped models.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-defined scope:** Evaluation excludes dashboard creation and mature enterprise BI deployment; questions come from English, publicly accessible Power BI artifacts (§§3.2–4).
- **Persistent failures:** Missing required information remains the most common classified error (§6.4).
- **Analyst inference—selection and transfer:** Screening for clear dashboard intent limits coverage of ambiguous user requests. Transfer evidence covers only the local Spider subset and one post-trained backbone (§4; §6.5).
- **Analyst inference—synthetic supervision:** Matching independently generated trajectories to synthetic answers checks consistency, but cannot guarantee that the synthesized answer correctly expresses the question. RL additionally uses tasks successfully solved by the teacher (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** Numerical tolerances and the scalar-match exception affect what “correct” means; error categories are assigned by GPT-5.2 without reported human agreement (§§6.1, 6.4).
- **Cost/latency claims need qualification:** The prose’s 54× cost comparison does not demonstrate matched accuracy, and some quoted costs differ from Table 3. Latency benefits are inferred from tokens and rounds rather than measured wall-clock time (§§6.2, 6.6).

## Open research questions
- **Can explicit completeness checks reduce missing-information failures?** These remain dominant (§6.4). Compare answer-schema and coverage verification against the current agent at matched inference budgets.
- **Does the approach generalize beyond curated public Power BI tasks?** Current coverage is restricted (§4). Evaluate independently curated private, multilingual, and non-Power-BI projects.
- **How reliable is synthetic supervision?** Semantic correctness is not independently established for every synthesized question-answer pair (§5.2). Audit a sample with experts and compare training on audited versus automatically verified data.
- **Do the efficiency gains survive full runtime accounting?** Wall-clock latency is unreported (§6.6). Measure tool execution, model inference, and total latency under matched deployment conditions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark improvements; **medium** in broader generalization and efficiency claims.

Material missing information includes complete training hyperparameters and prompts, inference stopping budgets, human validation of error classifications, measured wall-clock latency, and a comprehensive cost accounting including trajectory synthesis and tool execution. The announced code/data release was not independently checked.
