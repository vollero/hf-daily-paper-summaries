# When AI Reviews Train AI Reviewers: Scientific-Judgment Collapse and Mitigation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20942) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20942)


## One-sentence takeaway
In one controlled recursive-training step, synthetic peer-review supervision narrows rating and semantic diversity, while TrustReviewer improves agreement with official ratings through curated training and activation steering without establishing better scientific critique quality.

## Problem and motivation
Model-generated reviews can enter future training corpora, causing successor reviewers to learn earlier models’ judgments. The authors investigate whether this feedback narrows scientific judgments, a task-specific consequence not established by the general model-collapse literature they discuss. Their experiment isolates explicitly generated supervision rather than measuring naturally occurring AI assistance (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled recursive-training experiment:** Fine-tune Llama 3.1 8B on official ICLR 2018–2023 reviews, then train successors on ICLR 2024 mixtures containing zero, one, two, or three synthetic reviews per paper. Initialization and training settings are shared (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Empirical characterization:** Measure rating dispersion, same-paper review diversity, and corpus-level semantic spread; the authors name the observed narrowing “scientific-judgment collapse” (§2.3).
- **TrustReviewer:** Train directly on a curated corpus of 112,743 paper–review examples, approximately 1.9 billion tokens. Estimate activation differences between official and generated reviews using 5,000 calibration pairs, then steer the final decoder layer during generation; a separate validation set selects the intervention (§§3.1–3.2; App. C). The paper presents code and the curated corpus as publicly available.

## Evidence and main results

Recursive results use a fixed held-out set of 2,000 papers. Table 1 reports means ± standard deviations across three generation runs; score metrics exclude invalid cases.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Synthetic supervision compresses ratings | From 0% to 33% synthetic supervision, rating standard deviation falls from 1.63 to 1.44 and entropy from 2.31 to 2.14; both remain below the official-only baseline at higher exposures. | §2.3.1; Fig. 2 |
| Same-paper diversity decreases monotonically | Mean pairwise embedding cosine distance is approximately 0.159, 0.153, 0.147, and 0.142 at 0%, 33%, 66%, and 100% exposure: approximately 11% endpoint reduction. | §2.3.2; Fig. 3 |
| Corpus diversity also contracts | Paper-level embedding spread is approximately 0.609, 0.584, 0.580, and 0.579 across those exposures: approximately 5% endpoint reduction. | §2.3.3; Fig. 3 |
| TrustReviewer leads external baselines in rating agreement | Exact match: TrustReviewer 75.40 ± 0.46%, OpenReviewer 73.10 ± 1.36%, Qwen 61.85 ± 0.33%, Llama 33.93 ± 0.54%. Corresponding MAD: 1.079, 1.113, 1.335, 2.679. | §3.3; Table 1 |
| Steering gives a mixed improvement | Against the same unsteered checkpoint, exact match increases from 73.85% to 75.40%, entropy from 2.13 to 2.18; MAD changes from 1.077 to 1.079. Corpus spread increases, but same-paper diversity decreases slightly and remains below OpenReviewer. | §3.3.2; Table 1; Fig. 4 |

Exact match means matching **at least one** official reviewer’s rating; MAD measures distance from the mean official rating (App. E). These results establish distributional narrowing in the controlled setting and stronger recommendation alignment for TrustReviewer. They do not establish factual correctness, useful disagreement, or superior scientific judgment. Rating compression is not monotonic with synthetic exposure, unlike the reported semantic trends.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** One recursive step, one base-model family, and one review domain limit generalization; official reviews can contain errors and unobserved AI assistance (App. G).
- **Author-stated:** Embedding diversity is a proxy, and agreement with official ratings is not scientific correctness. Human assessment of critique validity and coverage is missing (App. G).
- **Analyst inference:** Curation’s causal contribution is unresolved: there is no matched uncurated-training ablation, and final TrustReviewer training differs from the recursive experiment in data coverage and training stages (§§2–3).
- **Analyst inference:** Reported variability covers generation runs, not demonstrated independent training replications. Unfixed decoding seeds, unspecified `top_k`, possible wrapper overrides, and unreported per-model failure counts limit reproducibility and comparison auditing (App. D; Table 1).

## Open research questions
- **Does narrowing compound across generations?** Only one step is studied. Train successive generations under fixed replacement and data-retention policies, tracking the same metrics.
- **Does diversity represent valid scientific disagreement?** Embedding distances cannot answer this. Have blinded experts assess correctness, specificity, and distinct valid concerns.
- **What does curation independently contribute?** Its effect is not isolated. Compare curated and uncurated corpora with matched training budgets, then apply steering to both.
- **Does steering transfer beyond this setting?** Evaluation remains within ICLR. Test fixed steering settings on another venue or field, measuring both agreement and expert-rated critique quality.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported within-setting findings; broader mitigation and review-quality claims remain less certain.

Material missing information includes curation ablations, independent training-replication results, per-model valid-output counts, exact numerical semantic results for Fig. 4, human quality evaluation, and multi-generation or cross-domain tests. Public artifact availability is asserted by the paper but not independently verified here.
