# MintAct: A Unified Visual Agent for Digital Environments

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.22083) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22083)


## One-sentence takeaway
MintAct combines screenshot-based grounding, navigation, and visual tool use in one model, achieving strong benchmark results while leaving fully joint training and seamless switching between UI actions and tools unresolved.

## Problem and motivation
Digital agents commonly specialize in grounding, mobile control, desktop control, web navigation, or visual tool use. The authors argue that maintaining separate models increases deployment costs, while combining them risks interference between domains. Heterogeneous environments also complicate RL: slow interactions, long visual histories, and unequal rollout speeds can distort the training mixture (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified model design:** A single set of weights uses screenshots and normalized coordinates for UI interaction, with domain-specific prompts defining available actions. MintAct starts from Qwen3-VL-Instruct and spans 2B, 4B, and 8B scales (§§3.1–3.3; Appendix B).
- **Staged training:** High-resolution grounding SFT precedes lower-resolution trajectory SFT. Per-domain RL specialists then generate successful trajectories for rejection-sampling fine-tuning, followed by joint RL **only on mobile and desktop** (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Controlled asynchronous RL:** Domain quotas and producer backpressure regulate the consumed training mixture; bounded queues, synchronization, clipping, and importance weighting address stale samples and inference/training discrepancies. The contribution is their integration for heterogeneous visual agents, building on existing RL frameworks (§3.4; Table 2).
- **Dynamic tools and synthetic environments:** Tool-registry-aware segmentation preserves the appropriate tool definitions during training. Separately, coding agents generate browser-rendered mobile and desktop environments for training and held-out capability evaluation (§3.3.2; Appendix A).

## Evidence and main results

Scores below retain the paper’s reported scale: §4.1 specifies benchmark “official metrics” but does not define their formulas or units. Online navigation uses a maximum of 30 steps; visual tool use uses 100.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Broad improvement over initialization | MintAct-8B versus Qwen3-VL-8B: AndroidWorld **67.0 vs 47.6**; OSWorld-Verified **48.9 vs 33.9**; Weblica **74.7 vs 55.5**; MM-ToolSandBox **24.5 vs 3.1**, using reported official benchmark scores. | Table 3 |
| Competitive, not uniformly leading | On reported official scores, MintAct-8B exceeds EvoCUA-8B on OSWorld-Verified (**48.9 vs 46.1**), trails MAI-UI-8B on AndroidWorld (**67.0 vs 70.7**), and nearly matches WEBLICA-8B on Online-Mind2Web (**39.1 vs 39.2**). | Table 3 |
| SFT stages are complementary, with tradeoffs | For 8B, both stages versus multi-step SFT alone give UI-Vision **56.9 vs 25.0**, but AndroidWorld **63.8 vs 65.5** and MM-ToolSandBox **19.8 vs 21.7**, on their official scores. | Table 5 |
| Joint RL improves the navigation average | From the same 8B SFT initialization, the mean of five navigation scores is **55.9** for joint mobile/desktop RL, versus **54.1** for mobile-only RL and **54.3** for desktop-only RL. | Table 7 |
| Final RL adds gains but does not preserve every score | At 8B, RFT → final joint RL changes OSWorld-Verified **43.1 → 48.9**, Online-Mind2Web **36.3 → 39.1**, and AndroidWorld **68.1 → 67.0**. | Table 6 |
| Synthetic transfer depends on initialization | Synthetic RL raises base-model AndroidWorld **47.6 → 60.3** and OSWorld-Verified **33.9 → 38.6**; from MintAct-SFT, those scores instead decline **63.8 → 59.8** and **42.6 → 39.4**. | Table 8 |

The evidence supports useful capability sharing and improved aggregate performance. It does **not** establish the authors’ strongest “no sacrifice” interpretation: some specialists outperform the unified model, and individual scores sometimes decline. No uncertainty estimates establish whether small differences are meaningful.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Joint RL across all domains remains resource-intensive and untested; growing screenshot histories limit long-horizon interaction; navigation and tool use remain largely separate; dynamic tool segmentation gives coarse credit assignment (§6).
- **Configuration inconsistency:** §3.4.3 reports 64 groups per update split equally between mobile and desktop, whereas Table B reports mini-batch size 48 and a 25%/75% target mixture. Their relationship is unexplained.
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** No component ablations isolate quotas, backpressure, off-policy corrections, or prompt-conditioned action sets. Quantitative throughput and utilization comparisons are absent despite efficiency claims (§3.4; §4.3).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation reliability:** Training relies heavily on model judges; synthetic evaluation uses the same judge as training. Independent judge validation and repeated-run variability are not reported (§3.4.2; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—deployment scope:** Compact parameter counts alone do not demonstrate on-device latency, memory use, or power efficiency; these measurements are absent (§6).

## Open research questions
- **Does joint RL across every domain improve the worst-performing capability?** Only mobile and desktop are jointly optimized. Compare two-domain and all-domain RL under matched compute and report every domain separately (§6).
- **Can one policy choose effectively between GUI actions and tools?** Current evaluation treats them largely separately. Test mixed-action tasks against GUI-only and tool-only variants (§6).
- **When does synthetic RL help rather than interfere?** Table 8 shows initialization-dependent transfer. Sweep synthetic/real training mixtures from matched checkpoints and evaluate held-out platforms.
- **Which infrastructure mechanisms deliver the claimed benefits?** Their individual effects remain unmeasured. Ablate quotas, backpressure, and correction terms while measuring mixture drift, throughput, and task scores (§3.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method summary and reported comparisons, but stronger generalization and efficiency claims remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes statistical uncertainty, baseline evaluation harmonization, detailed train/test overlap audits, identities of several proprietary teachers and judges, total training compute, deployment measurements, confirmed asset availability, and resolution of the conflicting joint-RL configurations.
