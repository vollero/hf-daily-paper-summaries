# OmniVBench: A Benchmark and Large-Scale Dataset for Omni Reference-to-Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.22069) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22069)


## One-sentence takeaway
OmniVBench introduces broad reference-to-video evaluation with factor-specific checklists that correlate better with human judgments than holistic scoring, alongside a processed training dataset whose effect on model training remains untested.

## Problem and motivation
Reference-to-video generation must selectively transfer identity, motion, style, structure, or narrative while suppressing irrelevant reference information and assigning factors to the correct targets. The authors argue that existing benchmarks emphasize content similarity and insufficiently test these compositional controls; existing training datasets likewise offer fragmented task coverage (§1, Tables 1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** OmniVBench contains 813 cases across seven task families and a reported 18 fine-grained tasks, covering content, motion, style, structure, narrative, multi-content, and cross-aspect references. Instructions intentionally avoid describing information that models should infer from references (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Evaluation protocol:** 12,172 manually verified, case-specific checklist items separate Reference Fidelity (RF) from Instruction Realization (IR), including factor disentanglement and target assignment. A VLM scores these checklists; separate automatic tools measure technical quality, aesthetics, and physical plausibility. Scores are normalized and hierarchically aggregated (§3.3; Appendix A.4).
- **Training resource:** Omni-R2V comprises 339,570 processed training instances. Task-specific pipelines construct reference–target pairs through cross-pair matching or inverse construction, followed by filtering and instruction generation. The authors state that they release the dataset and that it does not overlap with the benchmark (§4; Appendix A.1.1).
- **Empirical analysis:** The paper compares open- and closed-source generators, validates automatic evaluation against human ratings, and audits sampled training-data quality (§5; Appendix A.7).

These results support the usefulness of factor-specific evaluation on the sampled outputs and reveal different model strengths. They do not establish statistically significant differences between close leaderboard scores or demonstrate that training on Omni-R2V improves generation. The authors’ claim that the open–closed performance gap has “narrowed” is not established longitudinally by this cross-sectional comparison.

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper has no dedicated limitations section. The following are **analyst inferences from the supplied evidence**:

- **Training benefit remains unverified:** No training experiment or dataset-component ablation measures the utility of Omni-R2V (§5; Appendix A.7).
- **Temporal judging is sparse:** Motion and aligned structure evaluation use ten frame pairs at matched relative timestamps. Brief errors or absolute timing differences could escape assessment; the paper does not ablate evidence sampling (Appendix A.5).
- **Validation uncertainty is incompletely reported:** Human correlations lack confidence intervals, inter-annotator agreement, and task-specific breakdowns. Appendix A.6 details RF/IR human rubrics but does not provide a comparable VQ rubric.
- **Construction reproducibility is partial:** Source footage is primarily internal, and one camera-reference pipeline uses an internal camera-controllable generator. Processed-data provision helps reuse but does not make every construction step reproducible (§4.2; Appendix A.1.1).
- **Generalization and ranking stability remain uncertain:** Curated professional footage and generated references may not represent all user inputs. Table 3 supplies point estimates without repeated-generation variability or significance testing.

## Open research questions
- **Does Omni-R2V improve transferable control?** No training results answer this. Fine-tune a fixed generator with and without the dataset, controlling training budget, and evaluate by task family on source-disjoint cases.
- **How sensitive are scores to temporal sampling?** The fixed frame-presentation schemes are not ablated. Compare current sampling with denser or event-aware sampling against human judgments of brief motion and continuity errors.
- **Do checklist gains generalize across judges and tasks?** Validation reports aggregate correlations for one evaluation setup. Repeat with alternative VLM judges and report per-task correlations and uncertainty.
- **Which construction pipelines yield the most useful supervision?** The quality audit identifies weaker motion data but does not connect errors to learning outcomes. Compare matched training subsets from different construction pipelines and audit their downstream effects.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The resource design and tabulated findings are explicit; training effectiveness, close rankings, and generalization remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes training ablations, uncertainty estimates, inter-annotator agreement, complete per-model generation settings and failure handling, detailed VQ human-rating instructions, and source-level overlap-audit procedures. Actual release contents and licensing terms cannot be verified from the supplied text alone.
