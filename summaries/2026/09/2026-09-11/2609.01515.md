# TempCloze: Can Video-LLMs Identify the Missing Middle?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01515) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01515)


## One-sentence takeaway
TempCloze evaluates missing-middle video selection and finds temporal alignment to be the main aggregate weakness of evaluated Video-LLMs, with the best model reaching 76.92% alignment accuracy versus 98% for humans on a smaller sample (Table 2; Appendix C).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that text-based video questions and answer options permit linguistic shortcuts that obscure visual temporal reasoning. TempCloze instead asks models to select a video segment that connects observed beginning and ending clips, testing temporal compatibility through visual alternatives (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark construction:** TempCloze contains 1,521 videos from seven public sources. Caption-based LLM screening, quality checks, and optical-flow filtering select temporally coherent, moving scenes suitable for missing-middle identification (§3; Appendix D).
- **Controlled distractors:** Each video produces three four-choice questions. Semantic distractors use non-overlapping intervals; Alignment distractors shift or expand the true interval; Progression distractors reverse, reorder, or repeat its content. Candidates originate from the same video to reduce appearance cues (§3.3; Appendix A).
- **Evaluation and diagnostics:** The authors evaluate 10 proprietary and 21 open-source models, including two Seed1.8 modes, and introduce Mixed and Hard subsets for error attribution and sensitivity analyses. This is an evaluation contribution, without a new trained model (§4).

| Strongest models still trail sampled humans | Seed1.8 thinking achieves 88.58% mean accuracy and 70.81% all-three accuracy. Humans achieve 97% and 92%, respectively, on 100 sampled videos with two agreeing judgments required for correctness. | Table 2; Appendix C |

| Alignment remains distracting with mixed alternatives | On the 300-video Mixed subset, Gemini2.5-Pro scores 76.3% accuracy; selections of incorrect Semantic/Alignment/Progression options account for 2.7/19.7/1.3% of questions. | Table 3; §4.1.3 |

| Static and exact-boundary explanations receive limited support | No exact boundary matches are reported across 18,252 candidate clips. Replacing clips with midpoint frames reduces mean accuracy from 83.43% to 17.31% for Seed1.8 and from 74.92% to 19.07% for Gemini2.5-Pro. | Tables 5, 8 |

The results support an aggregate alignment difficulty and sensitivity to presentation under this benchmark. They do not establish a universal ordering for every model or source, nor a general measure of complete video understanding. Pass@k measures whether any attempt succeeds; it does not demonstrate that a model can select its correct attempt.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Fixed alternatives test exact temporal fit, with limited coverage of domains and camera styles. Open-ended generation, dialogue, audio-grounded inference, and unconstrained prediction are outside scope (Limitations, p. 9).
- **Analyst inference—shortcut controls are incomplete:** Boundary checks and midpoint baselines address particular shortcuts. The edge baseline reports mean ground-truth probability, not selection accuracy, so its near-uniform scores alone do not establish weak edge-based classification (Appendix B; Table 7).
- **Analyst inference—diagnostic generalization:** Behavioral analyses use four models on a subset selected for errors across evaluated models. Their trends may not transfer to the full benchmark (§§4.1.3, 4.4).
- **Analyst inference—causal explanations remain hypotheses:** Forward-ordered pretraining and diluted attention are proposed explanations for context and density effects, without direct mechanism tests (§§4.4.2–4.4.4).
- **Comparison ambiguity:** The claim that mixed alternatives are harder lacks a matched single-dimension comparison on the same subset. Reported uncertainty intervals are also absent (§4.3.2; Table 3).

## Open research questions
1. **Can models combine both temporal directions reliably?** Beginning-only context sometimes matches or exceeds both contexts (§4.4.2). Compare explicit forward/backward evidence integration against the existing prompt on matched videos.
2. **Does evidence selection improve alignment?** Longer spans and denser sampling reportedly reduce Alignment performance (§§4.4.3–4.4.4). Compare uniform and boundary-focused sampling at equal frame budgets.
3. **How much performance survives stronger shortcut controls?** Existing checks cover exact boundaries and limited static baselines (Appendix B). Evaluate candidate-only and edge-based top-1 classifiers alongside models on a human-validated, artifact-controlled subset.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Core benchmark definitions and reported results are clear, but interpretation and reproducibility have gaps.

Material missing information includes confidence intervals, complete per-model decoding and input-resolution settings, exact reorder/repeat generation parameters, a matched Mixed comparison, and verified repository contents or release completeness.
