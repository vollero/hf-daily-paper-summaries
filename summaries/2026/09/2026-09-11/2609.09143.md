# Studying Image Tokenizers as Visual Languages in Unified Multimodal Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.09143) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09143)


## One-sentence takeaway
In controlled Qwen3-based unified autoregressive models, caption-prediction loss is a more consistent cross-tokenizer performance signal than image-prediction loss, while reconstruction fidelity alone misses downstream trade-offs and interference with text modeling (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
Image tokenizers determine the discrete visual symbols a unified model learns alongside text. The authors argue that reconstruction metrics and generation-only or understanding-only evaluations miss how tokenizer choices affect joint optimization, cross-modal alignment, and text modeling (§1). They investigate how to interpret training losses across different visual token spaces and whether those losses explain downstream performance.

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled evaluation framework:** Freeze seven single-codebook tokenizers from IBQ, GigaTok, and modified UniTok families; continually pretrain Qwen3 models on text and image–text data; track separate text, unconditional-image, text-to-image (T2I), and image-to-text (I2T) validation losses; then evaluate after supervised finetuning (SFT). Main comparisons use 0.6B, 1.7B, and 4B backbones, with 256×256 images represented by 256 tokens (§§3–4).
- **Cross-tokenizer loss interpretation:** Establish task-dependent scaling and rankings, examine loss–benchmark correlations, and test vocabulary normalization for image-token losses. I2T uses a shared text vocabulary, supporting more consistent comparisons (§5).
- **Tokenizer design diagnostics:** Ablate discriminator type, semantic supervision, vocabulary size, and training objectives to study reconstruction–performance trade-offs, text interference, and image-token–word associations (§6).

The contribution is primarily an empirical analysis framework and findings, rather than a new general-purpose tokenizer architecture.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Loss scaling and tokenizer rankings depend on task. | Across data and model scaling, all four losses decrease, but UniTok favors T2I loss while GigaTok favors text-related losses in the illustrated comparison. | §5.1, Figures 2–3 |
| I2T loss predicts some post-SFT capabilities better than others. | Across tokenizers, hyperparameters, and scales, pre-SFT LAION-I2T loss has Pearson **r = −0.859** with GenAI VQAScore, **+0.877** with MJHQ log10 gFID, **−0.590** with VQAv2, and **−0.617** with GQA. For TextVQA, pooled Pearson **r = −0.025**. | Figure 9; Appendix C.4, Figure 21 |
| Vocabulary normalization improves within-family comparison. | Across three IBQ variants with 0.6B models, the T2I-loss relationship with GenAI-all changes from **r = +0.016, R² = 0.000** unnormalized to **r = −0.957, R² = 0.915** after division by log2 vocabulary size. | Appendix C.5, Table 10 |
| Better reconstruction need not improve downstream results. | GigaTok → GigaTok-DINO at 0.6B, learning rate 1e-4, batch 512: ImageNet-1K rFID improves **0.81 → 0.51**, but post-SFT GenAI stays **0.720**, while VQAv2 falls **52.25 → 51.31**. | §6.1, Table 3 |
| Semantic supervision improves the tested UniTok model despite worse reconstruction. | UniTok → UniTok-sem at 0.6B, learning rate 3e-5, batch 512: rFID worsens **1.86 → 2.23**, I2T loss improves **1.670 → 1.655**, and post-SFT GenAI/VQAv2 improve **0.670 → 0.690 / 57.21 → 61.28**. | §§6.1, 6.3, Table 3 |
| Image prediction contributes to text interference. | Reformatting generation samples into I2T largely removes text-loss differences within UniTok and IBQ pairs; removing I2T does not remove the UniTok text-loss gap. | §6.2, Figure 10; Appendix C.6 |
| Vocabulary size creates competing preferences. | Among IBQ vocabularies of **1,024 / 8,192 / 16,384**, the middle size gives the lowest normalized image/T2I losses, while the largest gives the best post-SFT GenAI and VQAv2 results at learning rate 1e-4, batch 512. | §6.4, Figures 13–14 |

These results support using task-specific losses as diagnostics alongside downstream evaluation. They do not establish a universal tokenizer ranking or a causal explanation for pooled loss–performance correlations. The authors’ interpretation that semantic supervision produces stronger object-level associations is supported by word-specific losses and PMI, but remains diagnostic rather than mechanistic (§6.3, Appendix E).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Results cover fixed-length, single-codebook tokenizers and Qwen3 pure-AR models. Other resolutions, token lengths, architectures, and backbone families require separate validation (Appendix E).
- **Author-stated evaluation limits:** Caption loss is unreliable for TextVQA; dedicated grounding, fine-grained spatial reasoning, safety, and human-preference evaluations are absent. Empirical higher-order entropy may suffer finite-sample bias (Appendix E).
- **Author-stated replication limits:** Central experiments use one data-shuffling seed; only one GigaTok configuration receives a second-seed SFT check. This does not establish robustness of every tokenizer difference (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** Cross-family associations may reflect additional differences: UniTok uses distinct cropping preprocessing, and Table 3 uses different learning rates between families. Matched within-family ablations therefore provide stronger evidence about individual design choices (Appendix A.4, Table 3).
- **Normalization boundary:** Vocabulary normalization is tested on highly utilized codebooks and does not calibrate architectural differences at fixed vocabulary size or establish validity under codebook collapse (Appendix C.5).

## Open research questions
- **Does I2T remain useful across backbones?** Only Qwen3 is studied; repeat matched tokenizer comparisons under another language-model family and measure whether correlations and rankings persist (Appendix E).
- **What happens when visual sequence length changes?** Fixed length leaves compression trade-offs unresolved; separately compare equal-image and equal-image-token budgets across token lengths (§3, Appendix E).
- **Does semantic supervision improve grounding beyond object words?** PMI and caption-loss diagnostics do not establish spatial capability; test matched UniTok variants on region grounding and spatial-reasoning benchmarks (§6.3, Appendix E).
- **Can losses guide compute-efficient tokenizer selection?** No from-scratch scaling study or compute-efficient frontier is derived; sweep tokenizer, model size, and data allocation under matched compute budgets (Appendix E).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported findings within the tested setup; broader generalization remains uncertain.

Material information unavailable includes a release commitment or location for this study’s code/checkpoints, full per-run numerical results, uncertainty estimates for each tokenizer comparison, comprehensive multi-seed replication, and matched validation beyond Qwen3.
