# MetroLLM-Bench: Evaluating Language Models as Transit Kiosk Runtimes

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.10016) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10016)


## One-sentence takeaway
A compact Qwen 4B PEFT student approaches frontier-model deterministic scores on this bounded transit-kiosk benchmark, but held-out uncertainty, shared templates, and configuration differences limit claims of equivalence or deployment readiness (§§3–6).

## Problem and motivation
The paper tests whether an LLM can implement kiosk policy from supplied rules while calling tools and producing an executable terminal state. Correct fares, consistent purchase decisions, and renderable outputs matter because mistakes affect passenger transactions. The author identifies a gap between transit comprehension or trip-planning evaluations and benchmarks that require committing an operational kiosk decision (§§1, 5).

## Approach and contributions
- **Operational benchmark:** 955 designed scenarios across six metro systems and eleven categories. Models receive a system-specific “framebook,” use six deterministic tools within twenty rounds, and submit a validated terminal state; validation errors permit correction (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Evaluation design:** Fourteen deterministic Tier 1 components are separated from eight semantic-quality Tier 2 components, six using a model judge. A system-stratified split reserves 717 cases for training-data generation and 238 for evaluation; human ratings calibrate the scoring stack (§§2.3–2.4).
- **Empirical study:** Twenty-six models evaluated, twenty-three ranked, a scripted baseline, serving-configuration experiments, and a four-size QLoRA sweep using 600 filtered teacher traces and multiple training seeds (§§3–4).
- **Reported assets:** Benchmark cases, framebooks, harness, reproduction scripts, and seed-42 student adapters and quantized weights are released; underlying scored files, judge caches, and training traces are described as author-archived and regenerable (Appendices C, G).

The evidence supports compact-model competitiveness on this scoring contract and a consistent adaptation trend under the tested recipe. It does not establish statistical equivalence, significant superiority over GPT-5.6, or real-world kiosk reliability. Composite performance remains lower for the 4B student than the cited frontier comparators, and category-level strengths differ (§§3, 6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Generalization:** Authors acknowledge shared training/evaluation templates, English-only responses, narrow adversarial coverage, and no assessment of payments, device integration, passenger satisfaction, or strict latency targets (§2.3; Appendix F).
- **Statistical resolution:** Most model scores use one run; category samples are small, and bootstrap intervals capture case sampling rather than combined training and inference variability. Principal comparisons were author-selected without multiple-comparison correction (§6; Appendix D).
- **Configuration and contamination:** Some configurations were selected on the ranked held-out cases. Serving builds also differ. Training exposure to public cases cannot be excluded for Muse and Qwen3.8 (§3.4; Appendices B.4, F).
- **Adaptation confounds:** The 27B student uses shorter training sequences; temporal examples are underrepresented, and teachers share the students’ model family. Other data scales and adaptation recipes were not tested (§4; Appendices C, F).
- **Scoring and data quality:** Calibration uses responses from one model run and reveals substantive safety-rubric disagreement. Retained case issues include ineffective closures, missing policy grounding, and ambiguous accessibility expectations (Appendices B.7, E–F).
- **Analyst inference:** The scripted baseline establishes an advantage over one fixed orchestration strategy, not over a fully engineered production policy engine (§3.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the student generalize beyond familiar templates?** Shared structures leave this unresolved; evaluate held-out template families and an unseen transit system (§2.3).
2. **What causes the 27B adaptation regression?** Capacity, trace composition, and shorter sequences are confounded; repeat with matched sequence lengths and varied data volume and composition (§4).
3. **How stable are the leading comparisons?** Single runs and held-out configuration selection limit certainty; tune on a separate development set and repeat inference on fresh cases (§§3.4, 6).
4. **Does benchmark quality translate into usable kiosk operation?** Integration and response-time requirements are untested; run an operator-reviewed kiosk pilot measuring terminal correctness, latency, and passenger outcomes (Appendix F).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides substantial methodological and statistical detail, but headline deployment claims exceed what its uncertainty establishes.

Material information unavailable here includes raw scored outputs and judge caches, the complete training traces and rubric prompts, repeated frontier-model evaluations, independent template-disjoint results, matched-sequence adaptation ablations, and end-to-end kiosk measurements. Public artifact availability was not independently verified.
