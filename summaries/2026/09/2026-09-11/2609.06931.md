# CARDEA: Auditable Reasoning Grounded in Spatial Evidence for End-to-End Coronary Angiography Interpretation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.06931) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06931)


## One-sentence takeaway
CARDEA combines multi-view angiography interpretation with inspectable bounding-box traces and improves zero-shot reporting after closed-ended reinforcement learning, but neither spatial faithfulness nor clinical readiness is established (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Coronary angiography requires integrating projections affected by vessel overlap and foreshortening, making interpretation variable across readers. The authors identify a gap between specialized systems producing fixed labels and vision-language models producing reports without spatial evidence linking findings to conclusions (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified inference pipeline:** A Qwen3-VL-based model selects keyframes and classifies views, then synthesizes up to ten retained images into dominance classification, binary complexity assessment, or a structured free-text report (§§2–2.2; Fig. 1).
- **CAG-specific grounded training:** Visual alignment teaches localization; self-distillation uses those detections and a dominance guide to construct Chain-of-Box (CoB) traces; RLVR rewards task correctness and, for study-level classifications, a parseable box conditional on a correct answer. CoB itself comes from prior work; the contribution is its integration and training here (§§1, 2.2; Fig. 2; §S1.4).
- **Closed-ended-to-reporting transfer study:** Report generation is excluded from training and evaluated across stages on held-out AngioCAD. Ablations distinguish the cold start’s effect on box size from the reward’s effect on box presence (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Released assets:** The authors report releasing model weights and inference code (Data Sharing Statement, p. 10).

| CoB training affects trace behavior without a demonstrated accuracy advantage | Separate fixed-budget, 400-step ablations: removing the reward reduced validation box usage to approximately **5%**; removing the cold start produced median normalized box area **0.76**, versus approximately **0.01** with it. | Sampling improves box coverage, with little task-score change | AngioCAD reporting, selecting the first boxed trace among seven rollouts: coverage **75.0%→98.5%**, two-class VS-F1 **0.686→0.691**. | §S2.1; Fig. S2 |

These results support transfer from the reported closed-ended training sequence to branch-level severity reporting and show that box emission can be encouraged. They do **not** establish faithful explanations, expert-equivalent reports, or clinical equivalence. The authors’ explanation that RLVR internalizes multi-view synthesis remains a hypothesis based on single-run training (§4). Statistical comparisons are exploratory, with no multiple-comparison adjustment (§2.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors—grounding:** Box frequency and size do not verify that boxes localize named structures or support conclusions. Qualitative examples were selected for box presence and, for classification, correct answers (§S3.3; §S4).
- **Authors—report validity:** VS-F1 collapses segment annotations to maximum branch severity, overlooking within-branch localization errors and additional lesions. Human agreement with MedGemma extraction, report readability, and blinded physician-report comparisons were not assessed (§S3.2).
- **Authors—evaluation:** Small complexity and keyframe test sets limit precision. Converted segmentation outputs, post-hoc expert binarization, differing frame-sampling protocols, and model-selected valid-view subsets constrain comparisons (§2.3; Table 2; §S3.2).
- **Authors—training and scope:** One run per stage leaves training variability unmeasured. Static images omit contrast-flow dynamics; other lesion features and procedural devices receive insufficient coverage. Offline frame-selection bias and the contribution of auxiliary mild-stenosis labels were not tested (§S3.1).
- **Authors—deployment:** No clinical validation; median end-to-end report latency was roughly **6 seconds** on **four B200 GPUs**, measured across **30 AngioCAD studies** (§S3.4).
- **Analyst inference:** CoB presence is best interpreted as an inspectable spatial claim, rather than evidence that the model actually used that region, given the reward and evaluation definitions (§S1.4.4; §S3.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Are CoB boxes anatomically correct and causally relevant?** Faithfulness was not tested. Next: blinded expert localization review plus perturbation of cited and uncited image regions.
2. **Does RLVR reliably improve reporting across training runs and cohorts?** Stage comparisons use one run and one external reporting dataset. Next: repeated-seed, matched-budget training with additional held-out cohorts.
3. **Does CARDEA improve clinical review?** No prospective or blinded reporting study exists here. Next: a reader study measuring diagnostic errors, review time, and report corrections with and without CoB.
4. **Would temporal inputs improve difficult cases?** Study-level synthesis uses static keyframes. Next: compare static-image and temporal-input variants on identical studies and controlled compute budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in this account of the supplied results; clinical and mechanistic conclusions remain limited.

Material missing evidence includes human-validated box faithfulness and report quality, training-seed variability, prospective clinical outcomes, and measured effects of temporal information and preprocessing bias. The text also does not specify the full model’s RLVR training duration or complete task-mixture proportions; released assets were not independently checked.
