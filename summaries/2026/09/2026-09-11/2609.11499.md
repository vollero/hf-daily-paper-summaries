# Recursive Code World Models: Building Complex Worlds through Recursive Scene Programs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11499) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11499)


## One-sentence takeaway
RCWM recursively reconstructs image regions as executable scene programs and revisits their composition, achieving better reference-view PSNR and LPIPS than three baselines on all ten tested references, with limited evidence about reliability and computational efficiency (Table 1; §5).

## Problem and motivation
Complex image-to-code reconstruction must recover small structures while maintaining scene-wide layout and relationships. The authors argue that whole-scene refinement can overlook details, while independently reconstructed parts can conflict after assembly; executable code alone does not resolve this construction problem (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Recursive Scene Programs:** Nested, editable subprograms encode geometry, materials, placement, child references, and shared dependencies (§2.1).
- **Recursive construction:** A frozen vision-language coding agent establishes each subworld, invokes the same solver on unresolved parts, then inspects and repairs the assembled parent. Decomposition and stopping follow visual judgment (§2.2).
- **Context-preserving local inspection:** Child calls inherit scene context and camera pose, with matching reference crops and projection subwindows. Shared errors are repaired at the responsible ancestor (§§2.2.2–2.2.4).
- **Implementation and evaluation:** A Three.js implementation compares reconstruction workflows using the same base model and tests construction order, parent revisitation, and recursion depth (§§4.1–4.3).

The claimed methodological novelty is the complete recursive solve at every level, including refinement after children return, rather than decomposition alone (§§1, 3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

These results support better visible reconstruction on the tested references. They do not establish correct hidden geometry, statistically reliable gains, or an advantage at equal computational cost. Additional-view renders demonstrate executable geometry, but have no supplied ground-truth multiview comparison.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Hidden geometry and absolute scale are ambiguous; visual inspection can miss errors; repeated refinement can be expensive; convergence is not guaranteed (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation uses a limited scene set, single runs, one base model, and only two ablation scenes; repeated runs and human relationship evaluation remain needed (§5).
- **Analyst inference—compute confounding:** Matching the base model and tools does not establish equal inference expenditure. Numerical budgets, token consumption, wall-clock costs, and budget matching across methods are unavailable (§4.1; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—restricted generalization:** References are stylized compositions and demonstration renders, with several related crops. Generalization to photographs or broader scene distributions is untested (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—completion ambiguity:** Appendix A requests completed hidden structure, while Fig. 10 states that crop-based scenes omit unseen regions. The extent and consistency of completion are unclear.
- **Analyst inference—measurement scope:** Spatial relationships and editability receive qualitative inspection rather than dedicated quantitative or human evaluation (§4.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does recursion improve quality at equal cost?** Compute matching is unspecified. Run repeated flat and recursive reconstructions under identical token or monetary budgets and compare quality–cost curves.
- **When does additional depth help?** Only one ablation scene actually descends beyond two levels. Sweep depth limits across scenes with varying structural complexity while holding budgets fixed.
- **Does parent revisitation improve spatial correctness?** Current evidence relies on image metrics and qualitative inspection. Compare otherwise matched workflows using annotated contacts, occlusions, and human relationship judgments.
- **How accurate is unseen geometry?** Single-view fitting and additional renders cannot validate hidden surfaces. Reconstruct from one view of scenes with known geometry, then evaluate held-out views and geometry.
