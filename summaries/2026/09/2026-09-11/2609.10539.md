# IdeaAMBIG: Benchmarking Implementation-Critical Gaps in Research-Idea Specifications

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.10539) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10539)


## One-sentence takeaway
IdeaAMBIG finds that identifying implementation-critical specification gaps is substantially harder than clarifying an already identified gap: GPT-5.6-Sol’s real-world clarification success rises from 13.6% to 80.6% when supplied the annotated blocker (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation
A plausible research method can leave choices unresolved that materially change its implementation. Working code therefore need not faithfully implement the intended method. The authors identify a gap between evaluations of research-idea quality and evaluations of completed artifacts: whether the intervening specification provides enough information for faithful implementation (§§1–2).

The target is **codification readiness**, not novelty or scientific merit. Routine engineering choices and explicitly open design choices need not be fixed (§3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnostic task formulation:** Separates readiness classification, defect localization from the specification alone, and clarification-action generation given the annotated defect. Actions either ask a targeted question or specify evidence to inspect (§3.1).
- **Evidence-grounded benchmark:** Provides 660 single-defect instances—163 real-world and 497 synthetic—with resolved counterparts, supported resolutions, and a taxonomy of three broad defect types and ten detailed categories. Sources include GitHub issues, reproducibility reports, and executed research projects (§§3.2–3.3; Table 3).
- **Validation and evaluation:** Combines human verification, annotation studies, evaluations of 13 LLMs, a controlled blocker-availability comparison, and oracle studies measuring specification quality and component-level implementation fidelity (§§3.4–4.4).

The contribution is a benchmark and diagnostic framework; the paper does not introduce a trained clarification model.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Readiness assessment remains imperfect | GPT-5.6-Sol: **67.5% real-world / 86.4% synthetic Macro-F1**, using 100 READY and 100 NOTREADY inputs per subset. | Table 1 |
| Localization trails defect-guided clarification | GPT-5.6-Sol: **9.6% / 12.2% Macro DRR**, versus **80.6% / 96.2% Macro-CAS**, real/synthetic respectively; 163 real instances and 200 fixed synthetic instances. DRR requires blocker recovery plus both taxonomy labels; CAS requires relevance, sufficiency, and no unsupported assumption. | Table 1; §4.1 |
| Supplying the blocker strongly improves the same clarification task | On the same real-world Task 3 set, GPT-5.6-Sol Macro-CAS rises **13.6% → 80.6%**; source-clustered difference **67.0 percentage points**, 95% CI **[59.0, 75.2]**, **p < 0.001**. | §4.2; Appendices D.2, D.5 |
| Taxonomy requirements contribute to difficulty | On 50 real-world instances, original joint blocker-and-label accuracy is **10.0%**, versus **40.0%** human-assessed blocker accuracy with taxonomy-free generation. | §4.2; Appendix D.3 |
| Oracle information improves generated specifications | On 50 paired real-world instances, GPT-5.6-Sol’s human-rated READY rate rises **14% → 98%** when given the defect, action, and gold resolution; difference CI **[72.0, 92.0] percentage points**, adjusted **p < 0.001**. | §4.4; Table 2 |
| Oracle benefits extend to bounded code components | On 20 selected synthetic instances, GPT-5.6-Sol implementations passing all predefined tests rise **45% → 85%**; target fidelity rises **30% → 90%**. | §4.4; Appendix D.4 |
| Human review supports implementation-specification scope | Of 100 reviewed instances, **94%** concern specification clarification rather than changing the underlying research idea. | Table 4; Appendix A.2 |

The strongest evidence for the authors’ bottleneck claim is the controlled clarification comparison, which holds the model and evaluation setting fixed. Comparing Task 2 directly with Task 3 is less decisive because their outputs and success criteria differ. The taxonomy-free ablation also changes both generation requirements and scoring, so its gain does not isolate a scoring-only effect.

Oracle results establish the utility of supplied correct information, not autonomous discovery or retrieval of that information. Executable results concern bounded components, not complete paper reproduction.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Instances are retrospectively reconstructed, evidence-resolved, and concentrated in AI/ML; they are not original early-stage handoff records (§3.2; Limitations).
- **Authors’ stated interaction limits:** Clarification is a single action. Multi-turn evidence gathering, conflicting evidence, and stopping decisions remain untested as a complete workflow (Limitations).
- **Authors’ stated multi-defect caveat:** The exploratory ablation uses 50 synthetic pairs with unequal response allowances; it does not settle performance on interacting defects under matched budgets (Limitations).
- **Limited execution coverage:** The executable study excludes large-scale training, private datasets, unavailable services, and complete pipelines (Appendix D.4).
- **Ranking uncertainty:** Five of six source-clustered comparisons between GPT-5.6-Sol and Claude Sonnet 5 have intervals containing zero; small leaderboard differences should remain descriptive (Appendix D.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Strict gold-target matching can penalize a different valid concern. The authors audit target uniqueness, but the supplied audit results are incomplete, preventing full assessment of this residual risk (Appendix E.2; Figure 17).
- **Analyst inference:** Automated semantic judgments remain a validity dependency. Human validation is reported, but its detailed results are unavailable in the truncated material (§4.1).

## Open research questions
- **Can agents obtain resolutions autonomously?** Oracle studies supply the answer. A next experiment could allow questions and artifact inspection, then measure fidelity, interaction cost, and unresolved gaps against direct implementation (§4.4; Limitations).
- **Does localization transfer to natural early-stage specifications outside AI/ML?** Current inputs are reconstructed and domain-concentrated. Collect prospective handoffs in other computational sciences and annotate gaps before implementation (§3.2; Limitations).
- **How do interacting defects affect recovery?** The existing multi-defect study has a response-budget confound. Compare single and interacting defects with matched output budgets and per-defect recovery scoring (Limitations).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central findings, but the source is truncated and this analysis does not provide complete paper coverage.

Material unavailable information includes detailed judge–human agreement results, complete target-uniqueness and multi-defect outcomes, some referenced statistical tables, and verifiable release contents. Deployment details provide general settings but do not fully establish exact model revisions or inference configurations.
