# An Open Recipe for IMO Gold: Training Nemotron for Olympiad Mathematics

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.10712) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10712)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors report an official IMO 2026 score of 30/42 using a natural-language Nemotron ensemble, with development experiments supporting complementary specialist checkpoints while revealing substantial verification errors and compute costs (§§5.3, 7).

## Problem and motivation

The paper studies how post-training, proof verification, refinement, and inference budgets affect difficult olympiad proof generation. Its stated gap is a reproducible, extensible account of these system choices, including open checkpoints, data, code, and resource accounting (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Two specialist checkpoints:** Both start independently from Nemotron-3-Ultra-GA. SFT uses 414,890 synthetic examples covering generation, refinement, verification, and meta-verification; RL uses 9,597 selected proof problems with asynchronous training (§4.2).
- **An integrated search and selection recipe:** Three checkpoints generate and refine proofs across up to eight rounds. Search acceptance requires unanimous full scores from 16 RL/SFT judgments. A separate panel supplies 48 IMO-style judgments per finalist for final selection (§§5.1–5.2).
- **Empirical system analysis:** Experiments compare complete single-checkpoint pipelines, verifier operating points, checkpoint diversity, and unsuccessful refinement alternatives (§7; Appendix A). The authors explicitly build on existing generate-verify-refine methods; the contribution is the trained specialists, evaluated configuration, and release.
- **Reusable assets:** The authors report releasing checkpoints, training datasets, code, submitted proofs, and Nemotron-IMO-Bench, containing 200 newly authored problems, alongside development-set assembly code (§2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Reported official gold-threshold performance | IMO 2026: 30/42 against a gold cutoff of 29; full credit on P1, P2, P4, P5, and 1 point each on P3 and P6. | §5.3; Table 1 |
| Large competition compute requirement | Submitted proofs became available after approximately 707M generated tokens and 1,464.4 GB200 GPU-hours; complete competition execution consumed 2.31B tokens and 4,784.6 GPU-hours. | Table 1 |
| Specialists improve complete single-model pipelines | On 30 development problems, after eight rounds plus fallback, aggregate jury scores were GA 162, SFT 165, RL 180, and ensemble 188. The ensemble was not compute-matched. | Table 2; §7.3 |
| Checkpoint diversity beats additional RL samples in one comparison | Under RL-only verification, RL 256 attempts scored 139 across all 30 problems using 1.59B generation tokens; RL 128 + SFT 64 scored 159 using 1.55B. Verification compute is excluded. | Table 4 |
| Strict verification exchanges fewer false accepts for many false rejects | On the enriched 300-proof audit, unanimous RL+SFT had 1.1% false acceptance [95% interval: 0.0–2.7] and 81.3% false rejection [75.8–85.0], relative to jury full credit. Relaxing to 14/16 raised false acceptance to 17.0% [6.5–27.9]. | Table 3 |
| Unanimity does not guarantee correctness | Of 25 accepted development proofs, the jury awarded 23 full credit, one 6 points, and one 0; all three checkpoints unanimously endorsed both non-full-credit proofs. | §7.2 |
| Additional search produced only unofficial improvement | After 8 h 25 m, a later P6 proof received 4/7 from independent human graders without official marking schemes; the corresponding total would be 33, not an official result. | §5.3; Table 1 |

These results support the reported competition outcome and checkpoint complementarity within the tested configuration. They do not establish formal correctness, general superiority over other systems, or a compute-controlled advantage for the full ensemble. Internal acceptance, independent model-jury full credit, and official human grades are distinct outcomes (§§5.1, 6.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated constraints:** Most main experiments use a selected development subset because full-benchmark evaluation was too expensive. Difficulty selection used preliminary GA performance (§6.1).
- **Authors’ observed grading failures:** Internal and external model evaluations both overestimated the competition score, approximately 32 versus the official 30. The authors attribute this to shared verification blind spots (§5.3).
- **Audit scope:** The verification sample deliberately emphasizes difficult, high-scoring proofs and reuses search-time RL/SFT judgments; its error rates are not estimates for the full candidate pool (§7.2).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** Single-checkpoint comparisons change generation, verification, and refinement together. They cannot isolate improvements in generation alone; the unmatched ensemble budget further limits attribution (§§7.1, 7.3).
- **Analyst inference—unisolated components:** The supplied experiments do not separately establish the benefit of the final-selection budget, each SFT trace type, or retaining GA for later-round diversity (§§4.2, 5.2, 7).
- **Comparability boundary:** Appendix A experiments use different pools and budgets; their outcomes cannot be directly compared with the main experiments (Appendix A).

## Open research questions

1. **Can verification reduce shared errors without rejecting most correct proofs?** Unanimous panels still miss invalid arguments (§7.2). Next experiment: compare verifier training or counterexample-focused prompts on fresh, human-graded proofs at matched judgment budgets.
2. **Does ensemble diversity improve end-to-end performance at equal total compute?** Table 4 controls generation tokens approximately, but the full pipeline comparison is unmatched (§7.3). Next experiment: match generation, verification, and refinement GPU-hours across specialist and ensemble runs.
3. **Which training and selection components matter most?** Trace types and final-selection compute lack isolated ablations (§§4.2, 5.2). Next experiment: remove individual SFT trace categories and vary final-panel size while holding other settings fixed.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** in this text-based summary; **medium** in broad causal or generalization claims.

Material gaps include full-benchmark results for the final pipeline, repeated-run uncertainty for aggregate scores, complete human grading of development outputs, total post-training compute, and self-contained RL reward details—the latter partly defer to external work. Artifact availability and official results are reported by the paper and were not independently checked.
