# EvoSafeHarness: Evolving Model- and Domain-Specific Harnesses for Securing Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.05903) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05903)


## One-sentence takeaway
EvoSafeHarness searches deployment-specific safety policies and executable controls around frozen agents, reducing average held-out DTAP attack success from 45.6% to 10.0% while utility falls from 83.1% to 79.8% across fifteen model–domain deployments (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Agents can cause harmful effects through both malicious user requests and instructions embedded in external content. The authors argue that fixed safety harnesses struggle across deployments: models require different enforcement strengths, while domains require different authorization rules and trajectory state (§§1–3).

Automated harness optimization introduces additional risks: blanket refusal, benchmark-specific shortcuts, and faulty enforcement code. The identified gap is a search procedure that jointly preserves utility, respects trust boundaries, and evaluates adversarial behavior (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Deployment-specific policy and code search.** A Designer edits natural-language policy and executable interception logic using a domain specification, archived candidates, scores, and failure traces. Victim weights remain frozen; runtime auxiliary models must use the victim model (§§3–4; Appendix B).
- **Security-specific review and feedback.** Prior defenses supply initial guidance. A fresh-context Criticizer challenges candidates with intent-preserving evasions; an Analyzer separates benign failures, direct attacks, and indirect attacks (§§4.2–4.5).
- **Nested evaluation.** Static checks, smoke tests, a bootstrap confidence gate, and broader search evaluation reuse earlier trajectories. Selection excludes held-out results (§4.4; Appendix A).
- **Empirical design findings.** Trace analyses show search discovering effect authorization, order-independent transaction constraints, concurrency-safe limits, and deletion of controls with no measured benefit (§7.1; Table A3).

The claimed novelty is this security-focused optimization framework, building on existing harness-search and policy-editing approaches rather than introducing those ideas independently (§2).

These results support improved measured trade-offs against the tested baseline implementations and transfer to the specified unseen workflows. They do not establish universal safety or deployment-independent superiority. Zero observed ASR is a finite-sample result; DTAP confidence intervals and paired tests are provided in Appendix H. Overlapping benign-utility intervals do not establish utility equivalence.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-identified residual harms:** misleading financial content and some direct authorization violations remain difficult when tool scope alone cannot certify safety. DTAP’s executable hooks also miss tool-free final answers (§7.3).
- **Author-identified selection risk:** small search samples can mis-rank candidates, especially for concurrency and action-ordering failures (§7.4).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** baselines are ported once and frozen, while EvoSafeHarness receives per-deployment search. CaMeL’s port approximates its original capability/data-flow mechanism, limiting conclusions about the original system or equally tuned baselines (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—generalization evidence:** controlled ablations use one victim and one search per entry. AgentDojo/AgentDyn use the `important_instructions` attack family; adaptive testing uses one target/runtime and lacks a matched adaptive undefended comparison (§7.5; Appendix B).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Figure 2 labels aggregate Table 1 numbers as model/domain-specific examples. Section 7.6 describes a 43.6% token reduction, whereas Table 4b associates that percentage with spending. Reviewer-model descriptions also vary between §7.5 and Appendix B.

## Open research questions
- **Does specialization outperform equally tuned fixed architectures?** Current comparisons freeze baseline ports. Next experiment: allocate matched search budgets to baseline configurations and unrestricted synthesis on identical splits.
- **Can final-response checks address semantic residual harms?** Existing DTAP hooks miss tool-free answers, and scope checks do not certify truth. Next experiment: add evidence-grounded pre-response checks and measure residual ASR alongside benign false refusals (§7.3).
- **How stable are gains across search runs and attack shifts?** Ablations report single searches and transfer covers a specified attack family. Next experiment: repeat searches across seeds, then test unseen attack families and matched adaptive attackers.
