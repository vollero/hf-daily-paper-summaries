# Memory as Plans: World-Action Modeling with Memory-Grounded Planning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11561) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11561)


## One-sentence takeaway
MaP-WAM converts episodic visual memory into language–visual plans for fixed-context robotic execution, reporting higher average RMBench success than listed baselines and approximately constant executor inference latency as history grows (§4.2; §4.4).

## Problem and motivation
Memory-dependent manipulation requires information that is no longer visible in the current observation. The authors argue that language summaries can discard precise visual details, while directly conditioning action generation on growing visual histories increases latency and memory consumption. Their proposed gap is a policy that preserves historical visual evidence for planning without repeatedly passing that history into the executor (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Memory-as-Plans decomposition:** Store completed segments as instructions plus sparse observations from actual execution. A language planner selects the next subtask, and a causal world model generates its visual plan from historical evidence. The executor receives a fixed-size plan rather than the accumulated history (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Progress-aware execution:** WAP jointly learns visual dynamics, action chunks, and normalized segment progress through flow matching and a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture. Progress also conditions subsequent predictions and triggers segment transitions; future visual prediction is auxiliary and omitted during deployment (§3.3; Appendix C).
- **Closed-loop calibration and caching:** Match the current image against nearby planned frames using RGB pixel differences to adjust progress. At transitions, append real observations to memory. Structured attention enables historical-prefix caching for planning and fixed-plan caching for execution (§3.4; Appendices C–D).

The claimed novelty concerns this integration of episodic planning, progress-conditioned execution, and calibration; the pretrained models, flow matching, and Mixture-of-Transformers architecture are adopted components.

These results support the architecture’s usefulness on the evaluated memory tasks and its executor latency advantage under the stated protocol. They do not establish uniform task superiority or constant end-to-end latency: timings exclude prefix prefilling, prompt encoding, transfers, and VAE processing, and planning remains a separate operation (Appendix H).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Memory organization and progress supervision rely on available segment structure; automatic segmentation remains future work (§5; Appendix B).
- **Author-stated:** The training-free visual matching metric may be inadequate in visually complex scenes (§5; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization evidence is limited by single-task executor training, one simulation benchmark, and two physical task categories on one robot platform. These experiments do not establish a generalist executor (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** One reported simulation seed and no confidence intervals limit conclusions about reliability across training runs. Baseline training and compute comparability are not fully documented (§4.2; Table 1).
- **Analyst inference:** WAP trains on ground-truth plans but deploys with generated plans; the supplied analysis does not separately quantify sensitivity to this mismatch (§4.1). Fixed executor context also leaves planner memory growing with completed segments (Appendix B).

## Open research questions
1. **Can automatic segmentation preserve performance?** The method currently relies on annotated boundaries. Compare learned segmentation with benchmark annotations while measuring task success and transition errors (§5).
2. **How robust is progress calibration to visual changes?** Calibration uses pixel differences, and the authors identify scene complexity as a limitation. Compare pixel matching with learned similarity under controlled lighting, viewpoint, and occlusion changes (Appendix D; §5).
3. **What is the end-to-end efficiency–accuracy trade-off over longer histories?** Reported timing excludes several deployment costs, while planner context continues growing. Vary episode length and memory sampling density, measuring complete task time, peak memory, and success (Appendices B and H).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and main reported results are well specified, but broader validity remains uncertain. Material missing information includes numerical content embedded in Figures 3–5, uncertainty estimates and multiple-seed results, full baseline training comparability, end-to-end deployment timing, and confirmation of code, checkpoint, or dataset releases.
