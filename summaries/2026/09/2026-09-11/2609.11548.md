# World in World: Explore the World with World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11548) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11548)


## One-sentence takeaway
World in World controls a frozen video world model through visual evidence inserted into native attention, achieving the best reported aggregate video-quality and camera-error results among tested rerendering methods on DAVIS and OpenVid-1M (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Exploring a recorded video from new viewpoints requires preserving event timing, placing visible content correctly, completing unseen surfaces, and recovering earlier generated appearance on revisits. The authors identify a gap in approaches that require separate conditioning pathways or additional training for different controls, and propose reusing a pretrained model’s visual-history interface instead (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared inference-time interface:** Convert source frames, target-view projections, rendered geometry, and retrieved history into clean visual states with camera, event-time, spatial-support, and activation information. Extract their K/V features through the frozen backbone and expose them through native self-attention (§3.1).
- **Complementary evidence construction:** Depth-based warping supplies target-view layout; reconstructed human geometry proposes newly visible surfaces; an archive retrieves generated states beyond the rolling cache (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Correspondence-guided attention routing (CGAR):** Persistent point identities, depth, and camera geometry guide queries toward corresponding source-video tokens (§3.5).
- **Evidence-wise attention CFG (EWA):** Independently regulate auxiliary evidence using complementary components of attention responses, with magnitude control and no additional denoising-network evaluations for guidance (§3.5).

The demonstrated implementation uses frozen LingBot-World 2.0 causal-fast. The contribution is the evidence interface and its orchestration; the backbone and reconstruction tools are existing components (§4.1).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Highest reported aggregate video quality | On the reported DAVIS/OpenVid-1M evaluation, VBench Overall is **85.192**, versus **84.295** for the strongest baseline, UniWorld-View. Overall averages seven dimensions. | Table 1; §4.1 |
| Lowest reported camera errors | WiW TransError **0.068622**, versus **0.068705** for UniWorld-View; RotError **2.8326°**, versus **3.1868°** for CameraAnything, the respective strongest baselines. Errors average estimates from Depth Anything 3 and ViPE. | Table 1; §4.1 |
| Best reported image-fidelity scores | WiW PSNR **23.1511**, SSIM **0.787205**, LPIPS **0.121664**; UniWorld-View achieves **22.4735**, **0.770554**, and **0.121668**, respectively, in the same evaluation. | Table 1 |
| No uniform advantage across video dimensions | Temporal Flickering is **93.711** for WiW versus **95.178** for ReCamMaster, with higher scores preferred. | Table 1 |
| Warping has the largest measured ablation effect | On DAVIS, removing target-view warping raises RotError from **1.7823°** to **6.1158°** and TransError from **0.053291** to **0.581847**. | Table 2 |
| Routing and guidance improve camera errors jointly | On DAVIS, removing both CGAR and EWA yields RotError **2.0346°** and TransError **0.056937**, versus **1.7823°** and **0.053291** for the full method. | Table 2 |
| Broader applications and memory benefits are qualitative | Examples illustrate editing, stabilization, motion transfer, geometry-assisted completion, and revisits after cache eviction. K/V sharing uses two instances of the same frozen model. | §4.4; Figs. 4, 6 |

These results support improved rerendering performance in the reported settings. They do not establish statistical significance: some leads, particularly translation error and LPIPS, are very small, and uncertainty estimates are absent. Qualitative applications do not establish quantified long-horizon reliability or transfer between different model architectures.

## Limitations and validity risks
The authors provide no dedicated limitations discussion. They acknowledge uncertain projections, unseen surfaces, ambiguous correspondence, and finite-cache forgetting as challenges motivating the design (§§3.2–3.5).

**Analyst inferences from the supplied evidence:**

- **Evaluation is difficult to reconstruct:** Selected video counts, exact subsets, trajectory distributions, and detailed frame-alignment procedures are unspecified. The reference images used for novel-view fidelity metrics are also unclear (§4.1).
- **Generality remains untested:** Only one backbone is evaluated. Human completion depends on reconstruction from sharp, minimally occluded full-body crops (§§3.3, 4.1).
- **Component evidence is uneven:** Geometry and retrieval receive qualitative ablations, without corresponding numerical results or stated revisit durations (Fig. 6).
- **Efficiency is incompletely characterized:** EWA adds no guidance NFE, but evidence extraction requires forward computation and archived per-layer K/V grows with rollout history. Runtime and memory measurements are absent (§§3.1, 3.4–3.5).

## Open research questions
- **How robust is control to geometry errors?** Projection and routing depend on depth and camera alignment, but robustness is unmeasured. A next experiment could perturb depth, poses, and tracks independently.
- **Does retrieval preserve dynamic event state over long revisits?** Evidence is qualitative and revisit horizons are unspecified. Test controlled revisit intervals with retrieval enabled and disabled, measuring appearance and event-time consistency.
- **Does the interface generalize across backbones?** Only LingBot-World 2.0 is tested. Apply the same evidence channels to another compatible frozen causal model and compare control gains and required changes.
- **Are the smallest benchmark gains reproducible?** No uncertainty estimates accompany close scores. Repeat evaluation across seeds and report paired per-video confidence intervals.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported rankings are clear, but their robustness and reproducibility are less established.

Material missing information includes evaluation sample counts and subsets, fidelity-reference construction, detailed alignment and camera-error normalization, guidance settings and activation schedules, retrieval budget and scoring details, hardware/runtime/memory costs, statistical uncertainty, and confirmed code or asset availability.
