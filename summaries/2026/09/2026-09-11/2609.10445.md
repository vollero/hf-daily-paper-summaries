# Building Multilingual Bridges: Data Mixing as the Pillar of Generalization for In-Language Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.10445) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10445)


## One-sentence takeaway

Joint supervised training on English reasoning, translated reasoning, and multilingual instructions gives Tiny Aya L2-Thinker above 93% average prompt-language reasoning on each evaluated benchmark under explicit language prompting, with modest aggregate accuracy losses except on advanced mathematics (Table 1; §8).

## Problem and motivation

Multilingual models can answer non-English prompts while producing English reasoning traces, limiting accessibility and potentially losing linguistic or cultural nuance. The authors investigate how to teach prompt-language reasoning without requiring extensive reasoning supervision in every language, while preserving task performance beyond mathematics (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Data recipe:** Jointly mix English reasoning (ER), translated multilingual reasoning (MR), and multilingual non-reasoning instructions (NR). Reasoning examples include traces; direct-answer examples use empty thinking blocks and mode tokens (§2.2).
- **Model and data assets:** Extend the 3.35B Tiny Aya base to a 32K context and train Tiny Aya L2-Thinker with reasoning supervision in English plus 44 translated languages. The authors report releasing model weights and multilingual reasoning data (§§3.1–3.3; abstract).
- **Controlled composition studies:** Vary language coverage, NR proportions, and English reasoning availability, then compare joint mixing with sequential adaptation and linear weight merging. The contribution is the empirical recipe and transfer findings; translation and multitask SFT themselves are established techniques (§§2, 5; Appendix G).

## Evidence and main results

Unless stated otherwise, Table 1 values average over evaluated non-English languages. Reported language compliance depends on an explicit prompt instruction (§8).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| High aggregate language compliance | L2 reasoning rates span **93.8–98.3%** across six benchmarks; evaluation collectively covers 60 languages, with benchmark-specific subsets. | Table 1; §3.4 |
| Small aggregate losses outside advanced math | L2-Thinker versus En-Thinker task scores: MGSM **68.0 vs. 70.8**; Marco-MIF **50.7 vs. 52.8**; GlobalPIQA **70.7 vs. 72.3**; Macaron **39.8 vs. 42.6**. MIST judge score improves to **85.8 vs. 85.3**, rescaled from 1–7 to 0–100. | Table 1 |
| Advanced math remains a substantial weakness | PolyMath weighted accuracy: L2-Thinker **11.1%**, En-Thinker **18.6%**, Qwen user-prefix forcing **40.3%**; weighting is `(2×medium + 4×high + 8×top)/14`. | Table 1 |
| Broader supervision improves held-out transfer | Moving from one target language to two regional languages to ten jointly trained languages raises unseen-language L2 rates: MGSM **14→35→60%**, Marco-MIF **10→13→16%**, GlobalPIQA **19→21→42%**; accuracy remains approximately stable. Results use region-balanced averaging. | §5.1; Figure 6 |
| Non-reasoning data helps both metrics | On unseen MGSM languages, increasing NR from **0% to 10%**, with MR fixed at **10%** and matched training steps, raises L2 rate **46→89%** and accuracy **49→67%**. | §5.2; Figure 7 |
| Mixing provides a favorable trade-off | In the controlled ten-language comparison, mixing is reported as nondominated: merging favors accuracy but weakens language compliance, while sequential adaptation loses accuracy and produces less predictable fallback languages. | §5.4; Appendix G; Figures 11–12 |
| High averages conceal language failures | L2-Thinker reaches only **23.0%** L2 reasoning on Croatian MIST-OEG and **43.0%** on Yoruba GlobalPIQA. | Tables 9, 11 |
| Shorter traces accompany less repetition | Authors report fewer thinking tokens and lower window-normalized 4-gram repetition than Qwen thinking-prefix forcing, except that hard PolyMath problems still require long traces. | §4.3; Figure 4; Appendix F |

These results support transfer of **observable reasoning-language behavior** within the studied multilingual backbone. They do not establish universally language-independent reasoning mechanisms, faithful traces, or accuracy gains caused by using the prompt language. The authors’ attribution of the advanced-math gap to missing RL is not isolated by an RL ablation (§4.2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Translation anchors reasoning style and cultural framing to English; low-resource translation may be unnatural. No human evaluation assesses trace coherence, fluency, or cultural appropriateness (§8).
- **Author-stated:** Without the explicit language instruction seen during training, the model may revert to English; ambiguous or mixed-language inputs remain problematic (§8).
- **Analyst inference:** “Unseen” means absent from MR, not absent from all training: those languages occur in NR, and evaluation targets base-supported languages (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Language identification measures predominant language rather than complete consistency or reasoning quality. Appendix H’s wedding example receives the lowest instruction-following score despite its favorable language-consistency framing.
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The claimed per-language translation cap conflicts with visible Table 3 totals—for example, French has **23,855** samples versus the stated **5K** cap. The supplied text does not explain this discrepancy (§3.3; Table 3).
- **Comparison limits:** External models differ in backbone, size, training, and language coverage. Token lengths use each model’s tokenizer, so shorter traces alone do not establish equivalent reductions in compute (§§3.4–3.5).

## Open research questions

- **Does compliance persist without the trigger?** §8 flags dependence but gives no quantified removal study. Compare no instruction, paraphrased instructions, and mixed-language prompts.
- **Do native traces improve reasoning quality?** Translation dependence and missing human evaluation leave this unresolved. Compare matched translated and native-authored supervision using blinded native-speaker assessments.
- **Can advanced-math accuracy improve while retaining L2 behavior?** The RL explanation remains untested here. Add a controlled RL stage and measure accuracy, language compliance, and repetition against the SFT checkpoint.
- **How much transfer depends on NR language exposure?** Held-out languages still appear in NR. Remove selected languages from both MR and NR while retaining the same backbone and training budget.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** Main results and several controlled studies are explicit, but the source was truncated, so this analysis does not provide complete coverage.

Material gaps include the complete training-data table, omitted translation and decoding details, complete seen/unseen splits, clarification of the sample-cap discrepancy, and run-to-run uncertainty; reported standard deviations primarily describe variation across languages.
