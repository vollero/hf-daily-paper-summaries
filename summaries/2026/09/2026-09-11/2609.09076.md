# ActReview: Rebuttal-Guided Training Data and Rubric Rewards for Actionable Peer Review Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.09076) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09076)


## One-sentence takeaway
ActReview combines rebuttal-derived supervision with instance-specific rubric reinforcement learning to improve revision usefulness, with stronger evidence for actionability than for technical superiority.

## Problem and motivation
Existing review generators often identify weaknesses without explaining how authors should address them. The authors identify two gaps: diagnosis and revision guidance are commonly conflated, and existing datasets rarely connect reviewer concerns to concrete revision actions (§1).

The intended application is pre-submission feedback that identifies a paper-specific problem and explains what to revise, where, and how.

## Approach and contributions
- **Separate diagnosis from revision planning.** Given paper context and a target weakness category, generate diagnostic claims, then condition revision suggestions on a claim. Unsupported categories should produce no claim (§3.1).
- **Construct rebuttal-guided supervision and evaluation assets.** ActReview-40K contains approximately 40K weakness–response instances from 15,819 papers. Aligned author responses guide synthetic initial-review-style feedback; rebuttal text is excluded from generator inputs. ActReview-Bench contains 1,000 human-curated instances (§§3.2–4).
- **Combine multi-task SFT with candidate-aware rubric rewards.** Qwen3-8B-Base undergoes SFT followed by GRPO. GPT-5.4 constructs frozen, instance-specific rubrics from references and diverse SFT candidates, incorporating hard constraints and weighted quality criteria (§5).

Training uses localized paper chunks; the main benchmark supplies full-paper context to all systems (§4.4). The distinctive contribution is this combined formulation, data pipeline, and reward design; rebuttal supervision itself also appears in cited prior work (§2).

| Local training context has a global-reasoning tradeoff | On 50 global-context cases, full-paper versus chunk-trained SFT scores 1.49 versus 1.17 for cross-section reasoning on a 0–2 scale; unsupported claims occur in 14% versus 22%. Both receive full-paper inference inputs. | Table 23 |

These results support improved judged revision usefulness within the tested setting. They do not establish that authors implementing the suggestions produce better papers, or that ActReview generalizes across disciplines. Independent human evaluation strengthens the evidence beyond synthetic-reference overlap, but small differences require uncertainty estimates.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope limits:** The benchmark selects rebuttal-resolvable concerns and excludes major unresolved novelty disputes, conceptual disagreements, and irreparable flaws. Temporal evaluation does not establish cross-discipline or cross-venue generalization (Limitations; Appendix F.2).
- **Authors’ stated evaluator dependence:** GPT-5.4 participates in enhancement, rubric construction, rewards, and evaluation. Human and independent-judge checks mitigate this coupling but do not eliminate it (Limitations; Appendix F.3).
- **Observed supervision and behavior errors:** Only 84.8% of 500 audited enhanced instances pass overall acceptability; unsupported inferred revision actions and poor localization remain. The model also overstates the necessity of particular revisions (Table 12; Table 22).
- **Comparison limits:** Specialized baselines are evaluated for out-of-the-box transfer from different training data and tasks, so their gaps do not isolate training-method superiority (§6.1). Negative pairs favor unraised parent categories and receive only a small human spot-check, limiting abstention conclusions (Appendix G.2).
- **Analyst inference—reporting inconsistencies:** §6.3 claims RL improves Task 1 specificity over SFT, but Table 2 reports a decline from 0.96 to 0.57. Main-text evaluation descriptions specify paper context, while the displayed pairwise judge templates omit full-paper input fields; human instructions also mention retrieved context (Figures 15–16; Appendix E.2).
- **Analyst inference—metric and ablation limits:** Specificity counts locations, acronyms, and numbers rather than verifying grounding. Reported suggestion-similarity values exceed the range implied by the stated cosine-based formula without an explained transformation (Appendix E.3; Table 2). Table 5 does not isolate candidate awareness from instance specificity using a reference-only, instance-specific rubric control.

## Open research questions
- **Do suggestions improve actual revisions?** Current evidence measures judged usefulness. A next experiment could randomize authors to feedback sources and independently assess resulting revisions and author effort.
- **Can prescriptions become less absolute without losing usefulness?** Overstated necessity remains common. Compare current rewards with rubrics rewarding conditional recommendations and multiple valid revision paths.
- **Can mixed-context training improve global diagnosis?** Full-paper training helps cross-section reasoning but slightly lowers resolution sufficiency. Test hybrid local/full-paper training on both localized and global concerns.
- **Will explicit negatives improve abstention on harder cases?** Training has no negative targets, and screened negatives favor distant categories. Add negative supervision and evaluate independently expert-validated, within-category distractors.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied tables support the main actionability finding, but reporting ambiguities limit stronger conclusions.

The source was **truncated**, including part of the independent-judge and technical-error discussion; this analysis does not cover the complete paper. Material missing or unclear information includes numerical confidence intervals, exact training hyperparameters and costs, complete main-benchmark paper-level separation details, the actual judge context payloads, and verified code/data/checkpoint availability.
